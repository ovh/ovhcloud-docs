## Objective

Network Attached Storage (NAS) is a file server connected to a network whose main function is the storing of data in a centralised volume for heterogeneous network clients.

## Requirements

- An IP address attached to an OVHcloud service (Hosted Private Cloud, Dedicated Server, VPS, Public Cloud instance, etc.)
- A [HA-NAS solution](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/storage-solutions/nas-ha/)

[[cpnav:storage-nas-ha-console]]

## Instructions

Click the name of your service in the list. The <code className="action">Overview</code> tab displays the **General information** of your service (including its **Identifier** and **IP address**), its **Service configuration** and its **Subscription**.

:::info
In the OVHcloud Console, the partitions of a HA-NAS are called **volumes**.
:::

To act on an existing volume, open the <code className="action">Volumes</code> tab, tick the box of the volume concerned, then open the <code className="action">Actions</code> menu.

### Creating a partition <a name="partition"></a>

To add a new partition, open the <code className="action">Volumes</code> tab and click <code className="action">Create a volume</code>.

Enter the **Volume name**, the **Size (GB)**, an optional **Description** and the authorised **Protocol** (<code className="action">NFS</code>, <code className="action">CIFS (SMB)</code> or <code className="action">NFS + CIFS</code>), then click <code className="action">Create a volume</code>.

### Modifying the size of a partition

To change the size of a partition, tick the box of the volume concerned, then click <code className="action">Actions</code> and <code className="action">Edit size</code>.

Enter the **New size**, then click <code className="action">Apply</code>.

### Change the frequency of snapshots

By default, a snapshot of your NAS content takes place every hour, and is saved on your NAS.

However, you can enable additional snapshots at different frequencies if you wish, which will also be saved on your NAS.

To do this, tick the box of the volume concerned, then click <code className="action">Actions</code> and <code className="action">Manage snapshots</code>. In the <code className="action">Snapshot policies</code> tab of the volume, click <code className="action">Manage snapshot frequencies</code> in the **Automatic snapshots** section.

Tick the frequencies to enable, then click <code className="action">Save changes</code>.

### Creating an instant snapshot

Apart from the snapshots taken automatically, you can create an instant snapshot of a partition at any time. In the <code className="action">Snapshot policies</code> tab of the volume, click <code className="action">Take a snapshot</code> in the **Manual snapshots** section.

Enter a **Snapshot suffix** (your snapshot will be named `snap-<volume name>-<your suffix>`) and click <code className="action">Take a snapshot</code>.

To delete manual snapshots, tick them in the **Manual snapshots** list, click <code className="action">Delete</code>, then confirm.

### Adding an access <a name="addaccess"></a>

In order to access the partition you created earlier, you will need to configure access.

:::info
Only OVHcloud service IP addresses can access your NAS (e.g. a dedicated server, a VPS, a Public Cloud instance, etc.).

:::


To authorise an IP to access the NAS, tick the box of the volume concerned, then click <code className="action">Actions</code> and <code className="action">Manage access</code>. The <code className="action">Access control (ACL)</code> tab of the volume opens.

Then click <code className="action">Add an access</code>, and select the IP address or IP block of your OVHcloud product in the **IP** field.

You must also define in **Type** whether the authorised access for this IP address is <code className="action">Read only</code> or <code className="action">Read/write</code>. Then click <code className="action">Add an access</code>.

#### Deleting an access

To remove access to a partition, tick the IP address concerned in the <code className="action">Access control (ACL)</code> tab of the volume, click <code className="action">Delete</code>, then <code className="action">Delete access</code>.

### ZFS settings

:::warning
The default ZFS settings suit most uses. Only change them if you know the workload stored on the partition.

:::


To change the ZFS settings of a partition, tick the box of the volume concerned, then click <code className="action">Actions</code> and <code className="action">Configure usage (ZFS)</code>.

In **Select template**, apply a usage template suited to your workload (<code className="action">Default</code>, <code className="action">Virtual machines</code>, <code className="action">Databases</code> or <code className="action">File systems (large files)</code>), or choose <code className="action">Custom</code> to set the *atime*, *recordsize* and *sync* values yourself. Then click <code className="action">Edit settings</code>.

For details on each setting and the values applied by each template, read our guide on [configuring the ZFS settings of a partition](/guides/storage-and-backup/file-storage/ha-nas/nas-zfs-settings).

### Deleting a partition

:::danger
Deleting a partition deletes all of the data stored on it permanently.

:::


To delete a partition, tick the box of the volume concerned, then click <code className="action">Actions</code> and <code className="action">Delete</code>. Type `DELETE` to confirm, then click <code className="action">Delete</code>.
