## Objective

You can terminate the protection of an infrastructure at any time. During the reactivation period that follows, you can still restore your data and change your mind.

**This guide explains what happens when you terminate a service, and how to reactivate it.**

## Requirements

- Have an active Managed Backup for VMware service.
- Be logged in to your <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Control Panel</ManagerLink>.

## Instructions

### What to know before terminating

| From the termination | During 35 days | At the end of the 35 days |
|----------------------|----------------|---------------------------|
| No new backup is made, and no period is billed after the current one. | You can still log in to your VBR, restore from the restore points that are still available, and reactivate the service. | The service can no longer be reactivated, access to the VBR is closed and your backup data is deleted. |

:::warning
**The 35 days are not a retention period**

During the window, your restore points keep expiring according to the retention of their Backup plan. A Standard backup, kept for 14 days, can therefore disappear well before the end of the window. If you need to restore, do it as soon as possible.

:::

- Terminating a service only affects one infrastructure: your other services and your subscription continue as normal.
- The current billing period is billed on what your machines reached before the termination (see [Understanding your billing](/guides/storage-and-backup/manage-backup/billing#in-case-of-termination)).

### Terminate a service

1. In the <code className="action">Dashboard</code> tab, in the **Subscription** card, click <code className="action">Terminate a backup service</code>.
2. In the **Terminate a backup service** window, choose in the **Infrastructure** field the one whose protection you want to stop. Only one service is terminated at a time.
3. Read the warning: it reminds you what the termination stops, what the reactivation period allows and what happens at its end. Click <code className="action">Terminate</code> to confirm.

Confirming does not delete anything immediately: the reactivation period starts. A notification confirms that the termination of the backup service for your infrastructure has been recorded.

Processing the termination takes about twenty minutes. Your request has been recorded: there is no need to send it again. The service cannot be reactivated during this time.

Once the termination is recorded, you receive an e-mail with the last date on which you can reactivate the service. The service then moves to the **Suspended** status, and the <code className="action">Terminate a backup service</code> link is no longer offered for it.

### During the reactivation window

- **Restore:** open the console of your VBR as usual and restore from the points that are still available (see [Backing up and restoring from the VBR console](/guides/storage-and-backup/manage-backup/backup-restore)).
- **Lost password:** the VBR password reset remains available (see [Reset the password of your VBR](/guides/storage-and-backup/manage-backup/manage-dashboard#reset-the-password-of-your-vbr)).
- **Track the deadline:** a banner on the dashboard shows the service that can still be reactivated and the number of days before its backup data is deleted.

### Reactivate a service

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Banner showing that the backup service can still be reactivated, with the Reactivate the service button" src="/images/storage-and-backup/manage-backup/terminate/01-reactivation-banner.png" loading="lazy" />

*The banner shows the number of days left before the backup data is deleted, with the **Reactivate the service** button.*

1. On the dashboard, find the reactivation banner: *The backup service of "`<infrastructure>`" can still be reactivated.* It shows the service concerned and the number of days before its backup data is deleted.
2. Click <code className="action">Reactivate the service</code>. The **Reactivate the backup service** window opens: it reminds you that the backups of the service will resume, and shows the number of days left.
3. In this window, click <code className="action">Reactivate the service</code>.
4. A message confirms that the service is reactivated.

Reactivation cancels the termination: the service returns to its previous state, its backup jobs resume, and the restore points that are still available are kept until the end of their retention. This is not a new order.

Billing resumes from the reactivation, and the days spent in the window are never billed. An e-mail confirms the reactivation.

### At the end of the window

On the day the window ends without a reactivation, you receive an e-mail. The service can no longer be reactivated, access to the VBR is closed and your backup data is permanently deleted. No backup is available any more, whatever your Backup plan.

To protect this infrastructure again, place a new order.

### Automatic termination of a service that was never used

This termination is different from the one you request yourself: it applies to a delivered service in which no virtual machine was ever protected.

- **Three days after delivery,** you receive a reminder e-mail if no machine is protected.
- **Ten days after delivery,** a second reminder tells you the termination date of your service.
- **Thirty days after delivery,** if no machine is protected, the service is terminated automatically. You receive an e-mail that confirms it.

Since no backup was made, no data is lost and nothing is billed. Access to the VBR is closed.

In this case, there is no reactivation window and no reactivation is possible. To protect your infrastructure again, place a new order.

You can then choose your virtual machines at order time, or set a tag on them in vSphere as soon as you receive your access details.

To avoid this termination, protect at least one virtual machine before the deadline (see [Protecting your virtual machines](/guides/storage-and-backup/manage-backup/tag-vm)).
