## Objective

The Anti-DDoS Infrastructure protects every OVHcloud service by default, with no configuration and no extra cost. Detection works from the shape of your traffic — packet rates, connection turnover, sudden changes in volume — not from what your application actually is.

That approach suits most workloads. For a VPN concentrator, a blockchain node or a live video stream, legitimate traffic has the same signature as an attack, and gets mitigated as one.

Anti-DDoS Profiles let you describe what your traffic normally looks like, so that detection can tell a legitimate peak from an attack before mitigation starts.

**In this guide, you will learn how to:**

- Recognise when your workload needs a profile.
- Understand what a profile changes and what it does not.
- Join the Alpha programme and request a profile for your IP addresses.

## Requirements

- At least one OVHcloud service protected by the Anti-DDoS Infrastructure.
- Your OVHcloud NIC handle and a contact email address.
- The public IPv4 addresses or IPv4 blocks carrying the workload, held on the same account.

## Instructions

### Why protection sometimes needs fine-tuning

Generic detection can fail in two opposite ways, and each one calls for the opposite correction.

| | What happens | What you observe |
|---|---|---|
| **False positive** | Legitimate traffic is read as an attack and gets mitigated. | Dropped sessions, broken tunnels, users cut off during a traffic peak, with no attack actually taking place. |
| **False negative** | Attack traffic is not aggressive enough to trip generic thresholds. | Degraded service during an attack that was never fully mitigated. |

False positives are the more common case, and a few workloads account for most of them: VPN tunnels, whose UDP packet rates run high; blockchain nodes, which use wide port ranges with constant peer turnover; live streaming and voice, which face sudden legitimate audience surges; and backups or large model downloads, which sustain bulk transfers for hours.

### When you would need a profile

Request a profile if any of these situations applies:

- Legitimate traffic is being mitigated and you have been working around it — forcing TCP mode on a VPN, lowering the MTU, or opening a support ticket after each incident.
- You are preparing for a known traffic spike, such as a live event or a product launch.
- You are running an application whose traffic is structurally unusual, and you prefer to declare it in advance rather than wait for the first false positive.
- An attack was mitigated, but not thoroughly enough, and you want detection to react sooner.

If none of these applies, no change is needed.

### What a profile changes

A profile combines two settings, applied together to the public IP addresses you specify.

**The application profile** describes the traffic your application legitimately produces. OVHcloud maintains the catalogue, so you select the entry matching your workload instead of defining thresholds yourself. The catalogue evolves as profiles are tuned against real traffic, so treat the list in the form as a snapshot rather than a fixed set.

**The sensitivity** sets how aggressive detection is for the IP addresses covered by the profile:

| Sensitivity | Effect |
|---|---|
| `low` | Fewer false-positive alerts. Select it when availability during peaks matters more than filtering depth. |
| `medium` | The usual choice, and the recommended starting point. |
| `high` | Even smaller attacks are detected. Select it when attacks have been getting through. |

Nothing else about your service changes: your Edge Network Firewall rules are untouched, your existing protection remains in place, and the price of your services does not change.

### What profiling gives you

- **Protection that fits the application.** Detection tuned to a workload catches more real attacks and mitigates less legitimate traffic.
- **Tuning you can request directly.** Describing your traffic becomes a setting rather than a support request.
- **Less operational overhead.** Fewer unwarranted mitigations mean fewer incidents to investigate and fewer workarounds to maintain.
- **Management above the IP address.** Applying a single decision to a group of services rather than to individual addresses is planned for a later phase.

### Joining the Alpha

During the Alpha, requests are submitted through the recruitment form. OVHcloud reviews each one, contacts the selected testers, and applies the profiling to your account.

Complete the [Anti-DDoS Profiles Alpha recruitment form](https://survey.ovh.com/index.php/143343?newtest=Y&lang=en). It contains nine questions and asks for:

1. **Your identity** — your OVHcloud NIC handle and an email address, so that the request can be matched to your account.
2. **The application type** — one profile from the catalogue. The current list covers AI and HuggingFace downloads, Blockchain (Solana), file transfer protocols, Media Streaming HLS, Media Streaming RTC, and VPN in IPsec, OpenVPN and WireGuard variants. Select `Other` and use the comment field if none of them fits.
3. **The sensitivity** — `low`, `medium` or `high`, as described above.
4. **Your reason** — whether attacks are going undetected, legitimate traffic is being misclassified, you are preparing for unusual spikes, or you want the best protection available. This tells OVHcloud which way to tune the profile.
5. **Where the application runs** — Bare Metal servers, Public Cloud instances, k8s/mk8s, VPS, VMware, Nutanix, or something else. Select every option that applies.
6. **The IP addresses** — a comma-separated list of single IPv4 addresses or IPv4 blocks to which the profiling should apply.

> [!TIP]
> Check the IP address list before submitting. Only valid IPv4 addresses belonging to the NIC handle you provided are added to the profiling, so a typo or an address held on another account is omitted without warning.

Once profiling is active, observe its effect in the [Network Security Dashboard](/guides/bare-metal-cloud/dedicated-servers/network-security-dashboard). Report any profile that does not behave as expected: that is how the catalogue improves.

### What comes after the Alpha

| Phase | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Alpha** (current) | Profiles requested through the form and applied by OVHcloud. Limited number of participants. IPv4 addresses and blocks. |
| **Beta** | Self-service management in the OVHcloud Control Panel and the OVHcloud API. A wider catalogue of profiles. |
| **General availability** | A stable Control Panel and API, availability in all Anti-DDoS-enabled regions for eligible IP addresses, and a defined migration path from any custom tuning already applied to your account. |

Later phases should also let a profile follow a group of services instead of a list of addresses.
