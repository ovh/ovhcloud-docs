## Objective

S/MIME adds two guarantees to your Exchange emails, using a personal certificate:

- **Encryption** ensures that only the intended recipient can read the message. If the email is intercepted in transit, or if an unauthorised person gains access to it, its content stays unreadable.
- **Digital signature** ensures the identity of the sender and the integrity of the message. It proves that the email really was sent by the expected sender, and that its content has not been altered between sending and reception.

The two are independent: an email can be signed, encrypted, or both.

S/MIME relies on a **certificate specific to each user**, containing a public key and a private key. OVHcloud does not issue S/MIME certificates: you order yours from a certification authority (Sectigo, for example) for the primary email address of your Exchange account.

**This guide explains how to publish your S/MIME certificate and how to sign and encrypt your emails in Outlook, in the OWA webmail, and on mobile devices.**

## Requirements

- A [Hosted Exchange](/links/web/emails-hosted-exchange), [Private Exchange](/links/web/emails-private-exchange) or [Trusted Exchange](/links/web/emails-trusted-exchange) service
- An S/MIME certificate issued for the **primary email address** of your Exchange account
- Outlook for Windows installed and [configured with your Exchange account](/guides/web-cloud/email-and-collaborative-solutions/microsoft-exchange/how-to-configure-outlook-2016)
- To encrypt an email: the certificate of each recipient, which your email client retrieves from the Global Address List (see [How encryption works](#how-encryption-works) below)

:::warning
**Start with Outlook, whichever client you plan to use.** Publishing your certificate to the Exchange organisation can only be done from Outlook for Windows. Until that is done, the other users of your organisation cannot encrypt emails addressed to you, nor verify your signatures — and the other clients (OWA, iOS, Android) cannot be used for S/MIME.

:::

## How it works

### How encryption works <a name="how-encryption-works"></a>

Each user has a certificate containing a public key and a private key. When an encrypted email is sent:

1. The email client retrieves the recipient's certificate, and therefore their public key.
2. The message is encrypted with this public key.
3. Once encrypted, the message is unreadable to any intermediary, including the Exchange server.
4. Only the recipient can decrypt it, with their private key stored on their device.

This is why you must already have the recipient's certificate to encrypt an email addressed to them.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Diagram of S/MIME encryption: the sender gets the recipient public key from the Global Address List, encrypts the email, and only the recipient private key can decrypt it" src="/images/assets/schemas/emails/smime-encryption-diagram.png" loading="lazy" />

### How the signature works

1. The sender uses their private key to sign the message.
2. The email client computes a fingerprint (hash) of the email content.
3. This fingerprint is encrypted with the private key: this forms the signature.
4. The recipient's email client verifies the signature with the sender's public key, retrieved from their certificate published in the Exchange organisation.

Any modification of the message invalidates the signature.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Diagram of the S/MIME signature: the sender signs a fingerprint of the message with their private key, the recipient verifies it with the sender public key, and any modification invalidates the signature" src="/images/assets/schemas/emails/smime-signature-diagram.png" loading="lazy" />

## Instructions

### Step 1: publishing your certificate from Outlook

Publishing your certificate makes it accessible within your Exchange organisation. The other users then retrieve it automatically to encrypt the emails they send you, and to verify the digital signature of the messages you send them. Once this is done, you can use the other email clients.

### Step 2: configuring your other clients

    #### Installing the browser extension

    Reading an encrypted email or verifying a signature requires a **browser compatible with the S/MIME control**, with the extension installed and configured.

    When the operation is not possible, OWA displays a message from which the extension can be downloaded directly.

    #### Declaring the webmail domain

    Once the extension is installed, open its settings and add the URL of the Exchange webmail hosting your mailbox to the list of trusted domains, then save.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="Microsoft S/MIME extension options with the Exchange webmail domain added to the trusted domains" src="/images/web-cloud/email-and-collaborative-solutions/microsoft-exchange/feature-smime/smime-owa-extension-domains.png" loading="lazy" />

    :::warning
    **The extension does not work in the reading pane.** Open the email in a dedicated window to use the S/MIME functions.

    :::
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="iOS">
    #### Installing the certificate

    The certificate is installed at operating-system level, in the PKCS #12 format (a `.p12` or `.pfx` file holding both the certificate and its private key, protected by a password).

    The simplest method is to **send the certificate by email to the user**: they then tap the attachment to start the installation and follow the prompts of the operating system. Export the certificate from Outlook beforehand (the <code className="action">Import/Export...</code> button used in [Step 1](#step-1-publishing-your-certificate-from-outlook)) and **protect the export with a strong password**: an unprotected `.p12` file exposes the private key, and therefore every email encrypted for you.

    Opening the attachment installs a configuration profile. Finish the installation in <code className="action">Settings</code>, then check the certificate in <code className="action">Settings</code> > <code className="action">General</code> > <code className="action">VPN & Device Management</code>.

    #### Enabling encryption in the Mail app

    The native Mail app supports S/MIME. Once the certificate is installed, open <code className="action">Settings</code> > <code className="action">Mail</code> > <code className="action">Accounts</code>, select your Exchange account, then <code className="action">Account</code> > <code className="action">Advanced</code> > <code className="action">Encrypt by Default</code>.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="iOS Settings showing the Exchange account in the Accounts list and the Encrypt by Default option in the account Advanced settings" src="/images/web-cloud/email-and-collaborative-solutions/microsoft-exchange/feature-smime/smime-ios-encrypt-default.png" loading="lazy" />

    {/* DRAFT: Reconstructed screenshot (HTML mock) built from the Apple documentation, not a capture of a live device: replace with a real screenshot from the product team. */}

    When composing a message, the lock icon in the address field shows the encryption state and lets you change it for that message. As on Outlook, iOS looks the recipients' certificates up in the Global Address List of your Exchange organisation — which is exactly why publishing your own certificate matters.

    For the detail of the Mail app behaviour, see [Use S/MIME to send and receive encrypted messages in the Mail app in iOS](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/102245) on the Apple website.
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Android">
    On Android, S/MIME is available in two ways: through the OWA webmail from the device browser, or through an email client that supports S/MIME for Exchange.

    #### Using the OWA webmail

    This is the most direct route and it requires no installation: open [the OWA webmail](#step-2-configuring-your-other-clients) from your device browser to read and send signed and encrypted messages.

    #### Using a compatible email client

    The certificate installs at system level, from <code className="action">Settings</code> > <code className="action">Security</code> > <code className="action">Encryption & credentials</code> (menu names vary by manufacturer and Android version).

    Once that is done, the certificate is available to the system. It is then the email application that determines whether you can sign and encrypt: pick one that explicitly advertises S/MIME support for Exchange.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="Diagram of S/MIME support on Android: the certificate installs in the system credential store, then the email application determines whether signing and encryption are available" src="/images/assets/schemas/emails/smime-android-support-diagram.png" loading="lazy" />

    :::info
    The **Gmail** app supports S/MIME through a managed Google Workspace organisation. With an OVHcloud Exchange mailbox, use the OWA webmail or a compatible third-party client instead.

    :::

    #### Outlook for Android

    Microsoft documents S/MIME for Outlook mobile **for Exchange Online**: the feature relies on a virtual certificate collection configured on the Exchange Online side, and automated certificate delivery relies on Microsoft Intune as the enrollment provider. These prerequisites apply to Exchange Online rather than to mailboxes hosted on the OVHcloud Exchange platform.

    See [S/MIME for Outlook for iOS and Android in Exchange Online](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/exchange/clients-and-mobile-in-exchange-online/outlook-for-ios-and-android/smime-outlook-for-ios-and-android) on the Microsoft website for the full prerequisites.

## Limitations

- To encrypt an email, you must already have the recipient's certificate.
- Losing the device or the certificates means **losing access to encrypted emails** if no backup exists. Keep a backup copy of your certificate in a safe place.
- S/MIME is configured on each client separately. On mobile, the iOS Mail app supports it natively; on Android, use the OWA webmail or an email client that supports S/MIME for Exchange (see [Step 2](#step-2-configuring-your-other-clients)).
- If a transport rule modifies the body of the email, the signed email is shown as an attachment in the recipient's Outlook.
