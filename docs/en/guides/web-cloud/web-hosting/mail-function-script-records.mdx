## Objective

Automated emails are messages sent via scripts, usually through PHP's `mail()` function. For example, they are used for your website's contact form, allowing your visitors to send you messages.

:::info
This guide mainly applies to emails sent from scripts located on your [OVHcloud web hosting plan](/links/web/hosting) using the PHP "mail()" function.

If you would like to manage the email accounts included in your MX Plan service or in your [OVHcloud web hosting plan](/links/web/hosting), please refer to our [MX Plan documentation](/guides/web-cloud/email-and-collaborative-solutions/mx-plan/landing-page-mx-plan).
:::

:::tip
This guide covers sending via the PHP "mail()" function, which is monitored from the OVHcloud Console. You can also send emails from your shared hosting through an [authenticated SMTP connection](#SMTP) with an email account attached to your domain. To choose between the two methods and configure SMTP sending, refer to the guide [Web Hosting - Email sending best practices](/guides/web-cloud/web-hosting/email-sending-best-practices).
:::

**This guide explains how to monitor and manage automated emails sent from your OVHcloud web hosting plan.**

    The page shows the automated email activity generated by your scripts:

    - **Service status** (**General information** section): The current status of the service sending automated emails from your web hosting plan:
        - If it is green (*"Sending active"* or *"Sending active (forced)"*), it means that outgoing messages are operational.
        - If it is red (*"Sending blocked (error)"*, *"Sending blocked (bounces)"* or *"Sending blocked (spam)"*), the sending has stopped. For an automatic block (bounces or spam), a message at the top of the page specifies the reason.

        Depending on this status, the outgoing process will be different.

    - **Error reports to**: Receive a daily report at the email address of your choice. Set it using the <code className="action">Set the recipient</code> link (or <code className="action">Change recipient</code> if an address has already been entered). The report includes emails sent from your web hosting plan that bounced back with an error at OVHcloud.
    - **Emails sent today**: Automated emails sent today.
    - **Emails with errors**: The number of automated emails sent from your web hosting plan that returned an error to OVHcloud.
    - **Detected impersonations**: The number of emails detected as impersonating a sender identity.
    - **Total for period**: The number of emails sent and a chart of their history over the last 7, 30 or 90 days.

    When the **Emails with errors** or **Detected impersonations** counter is not zero, the <code className="action">View emails</code> link opens the logs of the most recent send failures. Here, you will find the email addresses concerned, with the associated error. Warning: this log will not be reset, even if you decide to <code className="action">Purge emails</code> or <code className="action">Unblock sending</code>.

    To the right of the **Service status**, the <code className="action">⋮</code> button lets you manage automated email sending from your web hosting plan. Depending on the service status, some actions may not be available: they then remain greyed out.

    - **Block sending** or **Unblock sending**, depending on the service status: Blocks the distribution of automated emails from your web hosting plan (emails generated by your scripts after the blocking will not be sent, but will be stored in a queue for a maximum of 72 hours), or restores it (emails in the queue are then distributed again).
    - **Purge emails**: Clears the emails in the queue and unblocks the sending of emails. This action is only available when sending is blocked. For confidentiality, OVHcloud cannot access queued emails. You can only view these emails if they have been saved in your website's database before being sent.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="Service status actions menu with Unblock sending and Purge emails" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/web-hosting/email-scripts/actions-menu.png" loading="lazy" />

    To perform the desired action, click the corresponding entry, then confirm with the button that repeats the name of the action. In some cases, the change may take several minutes to take effect.

</Tabs>

:::info
To avoid unwanted use of automated emails from your web hosting plan, we strongly recommend setting up a security system, such as including a "Captcha" in the forms on your website that can send emails (a contact form, for example).
:::

If you notice that the emails generated from your scripts are no longer sent, but the service status still allows the emails to be sent (*"Sending active"* or *"Sending active (forced)"*), check the following:

- **Check the scripts sending the email**: The scripts may not be able to send the emails due to a syntax error. Check the content of your scripts, correct them if necessary, then try again.

- **Check email sending via test script**: Create a test script that sends an email to your personal email address using the following code:

```php
<?php
$to = "RecipientEmail@address.tld"; 
$subject = "Test mail PHP"; 
$content = "The body/content of the email";
$headers = "From: Website <SendingEmail@address.tld>\r\nReply-To: SendingEmail@address.tld";

if (mail($to, $subject, $content, $headers))
echo "The email has been sent successfully!";
else
echo "Email sending failed!";
?>
```

For `$headers`, enter the same sending email address twice.

If you receive the message *The email has been sent successfully!* on the email address you have defined in the line for `$to`, this indicates that the scripts sending your emails contain errors.

- **Make sure that your script actually uses the "mail()" function**: the checks and statistics on this page only apply to emails sent via "mail()". If your script, or the email settings of your website, specify an SMTP server, the emails go through that server and do not appear here. See the [Sending emails using an SMTP script](#SMTP) section.

- **Check the total size of your email**: The email sent must not exceed the total size of **10 MB** (encapsulation and header included). The content of your email must not exceed **7/8 MB**.

### Managing the "Sending blocked (error)", "Sending blocked (bounces)" and "Sending blocked (spam)" states <a name="block-state"></a>

In this section, you will find details on each status that has blocked your email function.

:::warning
Before detailing each of these statuses, it is important to understand the points that can degrade your domain name's reputation or prevent your emails from being received.

Verify the following upstream:

- The configuration of [the SPF record](/guides/web-cloud/domains/dns-zone-spf) in the domain name's DNS zone.
- The configuration of [the DMARC record](/guides/web-cloud/domains/dns-zone-dmarc) in the DNS zone of the domain name, **only if the destination server requires it**.
- Check the reputation of the IP address that sent the email ([here the IP of your web hosting plan](/guides/web-cloud/web-hosting/clusters-and-shared-hosting-ip)), using tools such as [MXtoolbox](https://mxtoolbox.com/) or [Spamhaus](https://check.spamhaus.org/).
- The email does not contain any elements that could be interpreted as spam. You can find a non-exhaustive list of these elements in the "[Case 3: Sending legitimate emails classified as spam](#elements-list-spam)" section of this guide.
- If there is no blockage on the OVHcloud side, and if the email has not been received or rejected by the recipient, contact the recipient to have them check if the email has been blocked on the receiving server.

:::

This state occurs when:

- Too many emails were sent in quick succession.
- Too many emails are returned in error.
- You have blocked sending yourself from the <ManagerLink to="/beta/#/web-cloud/hosting">OVHcloud Console</ManagerLink>.

- **Case 3: Sending of legitimate emails considered as spam** <a name="elements-list-spam"></a>

If you are the originator of the emails that caused the block, below are some examples of **practices to avoid** when sending an email (so that it is not immediately considered spam):

- 3 or more words in upper case in the subject of the email.
- Email with empty subject/body.
- The email only contains an image larger than 1 MB and very little text.
- The subject of the email starts with: Hi, FREE, BUY, BUYING, or similar phrasing.
- The email contains more than 70% "white space" (abuse of the "SPACE" or "ENTER" key on the keyboard).
- The font size used to write the email is extremely large.
- The text colour and the background colour are the same.
- The sender IP address (IP of your Internet access point for example) has a negative reputation with spam protection services.
- The header of the sent email does not respect the RFC email standards.
- Links in the email are incorrect.
- A URL in the email is not secure (example: a URL written as `https://`, while the actual URL only exists as `http://`).
- The email contains terms of a pornographic nature or similar.
- The email contains an executable (EXE, BAT, PIF, XML, XLSX or documents with macros); this applies even if the file is inside a compressed archive (.zip, .rar, etc.).

If your service is still in a *"Sending blocked (spam)"* state, reply to the automatic email you received, specifying that you have taken the necessary measures.

Our antispam service will analyse the situation, and our support team will get back to you to explain the unblock procedure.

### Sending emails using an "SMTP" script <a name="SMTP"></a>

[[fragment:support-scope]]

Shared hosting also allows you to send emails through a script that uses an authenticated SMTP connection with an email account attached to your domain, instead of the "mail()" function. The sending quota then depends on the email account (around 200 emails per hour for an MX Plan account) and on the source IP address (300 emails per hour per IP address, shared with the other websites of your hosting cluster), and the emails are not visible in the <code className="action">Email scripts</code> tab of the OVHcloud Console.

To compare the two methods and configure SMTP sending (server, port, PHPMailer example, CMS plugins), refer to the guide [Web Hosting - Email sending best practices](/guides/web-cloud/web-hosting/email-sending-best-practices).
