## Objective

With an MX record, you can link a domain name to the server on your email platform. It is essential for the sender's email service to reach the recipient's email service.

**Find out how to configure an MX record for your domain name at OVHcloud.**

## Instructions

### Understanding the role of MX records

The MX (**M**ail e**X**change) record is a type of DNS record that determines which receiving email servers are attached to your domain name.

To understand how it works, we will use an example:

- The address **sender@otherdomain.tld** sends an email to **contact@domain.tld**.
- The sending email server (**Outgoing mail server**) queries the DNS zone for the domain name **domain.tld** and reads the **MX** records.
- The email is forwarded to the URL of the **MX** record that was read.

<Region zones={['eu']}>

- The email is sent to the target **mx0.mail.ovh.net**, which is preceded by the value **0**. This value corresponds to the priority: the lowest value is queried first and the highest last. This means that having multiple MX records helps compensate for a lack of response from the server designated by the record with the lowest priority, by moving on to subsequent servers in order of priority.

</Region>

<Region zones={['ca', 'apac']}>

- The email is sent to the target **mx0.mail.ovh.ca**, which is preceded by the value **0**. This value corresponds to the priority: the lowest value is queried first and the highest last. This means that having multiple MX records helps compensate for a lack of response from the server designated by the record with the lowest priority, by moving on to subsequent servers in order of priority.

</Region>

<img className="thumbnail" alt="email" src="/images/assets/schemas/emails/mx-dns-resolution.png" loading="lazy" />

You can set up multiple MX records for the same domain name. It is then necessary to define a *priority* number for each of them. MX records are queried in ascending order from lowest number to highest number until a response is received from the receiving server.

:::warning
Generally speaking, **modifying MX records in a domain name's DNS zone warrants caution**. If you make any mistakes configuring the records, it may make it impossible for emails to reach your email address. Please take care when you carry out this procedure.
If you have any doubts, we advise contacting a [specialist provider](/links/partner).

:::

### Configuring an MX record in an OVHcloud DNS zone

Click the tabs below to view each of the **5** steps.

<Tabs>
  <Tab label="Step 1">
    Go to the <ManagerLink to="/beta/#/web-cloud/domains/listing">Domains & DNS</ManagerLink> page, then choose the domain name concerned.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="Domains & DNS page and list of the account's domain names" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/domain-dns/listing.png" loading="lazy" />
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Step 2">
    Click the <code className="action">DNS Zone</code> tab. Your records are listed in a table, with one row per record.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="DNS Zone tab of a domain name and records table" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/domain-dns/dns-zone/zone-overview.png" loading="lazy" />
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Step 3">
    First check whether MX records already exist: click <code className="action">Filters</code> above the table, then select <code className="action">MX</code> in the **Record type** list, or use the **Search** field.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="DNS zone filtered on MX records" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/domain-dns/dns-zone/filter-mx.png" loading="lazy" />
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Step 4">
    - **To modify an existing MX record**, on its row, click the <code className="action">⋮</code> button in the **Actions** column, then <code className="action">Modify</code>. The row expands into an edit form.

      <img className="thumbnail" alt="Actions menu of an MX record in the DNS zone" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/domain-dns/dns-zone/mx-row-actions.png" loading="lazy" />

    - **To add a new MX record**, click <code className="action">Add an entry</code> above the table. In the form that opens, select `MX — Mail server` in the **Type** list.

      <img className="thumbnail" alt="Type list of the Add an entry form, MX type" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/domain-dns/dns-zone/add-entry-type-list-mx.png" loading="lazy" />
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Step 5">
    Fill in the fields according to the email solution you use:

    - **If you have an OVHcloud email solution**, use the values provided in the "[OVHcloud MX configuration values](#mxovhcloud)" section above: `@` in the **Subdomain** field, then the **Priority** and the **Target (mail server)** of each record.
    - **If you have another email solution**, refer to the information provided by your email service provider.

    Fill in the **TTL** (leave it on `Default` or choose `Custom`), then click <code className="action">Add</code> (or <code className="action">Modify</code> for an existing record). The form reminds you that manually editing MX fields may result in email loss: check your values before confirming.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="Add an entry form of the DNS zone, MX record to mx0.mail.ovh.net" src="/images/assets/screens/console/product-selection/web-cloud/domain-dns/dns-zone/add-mx-record.png" loading="lazy" />
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

:::info
The change can take between 4 and 24 hours to propagate fully.

:::
