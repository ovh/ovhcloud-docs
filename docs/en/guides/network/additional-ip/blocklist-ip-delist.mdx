## Objective

Blocklisting (also known as blacklisting, or being listed on a Realtime Blackhole List (RBL)) is a spam-fighting measure applied to IP addresses or IP ranges to block email that is considered spam or that may contain malware. When an email domain or IP address is blocklisted, messages sent from it may not reach their recipients, which affects deliverability and sender reputation. Messages that are delivered may land in the recipient's spam folder rather than their inbox.

Blocklists can include domain names and IP addresses that pose no actual threat. Some spam-filtering services, such as SpamRATS, also take reverse DNS into account when evaluating IP addresses.

:::info
For the best practices on running an email server, refer to our guide on [how to prevent your emails from being marked as spam](/guides/bare-metal-cloud/dedicated-servers/mail-sending-optimization).

:::


**This guide explains the actions you can take to delist OVHcloud IP addresses that appear on a blocklist.**

:::warning
The information in this guide is subject to change and applies to newly purchased IP addresses. OVHcloud cannot be held responsible for the actions of third-party providers.

We recommend that you contact a [specialist service provider](/links/partner) or our [community of users](/links/community) if you have any difficulties or doubts concerning the administration, use, or implementation of services on a server.

:::


## Requirements

- You have no open abuse cases involving your services.

## Instructions

### Supported providers

- [Spamhaus](https://check.spamhaus.org/)

    - [Spamhaus Block List (SBL)](https://www.spamhaus.org/blocklists/spamhaus-blocklist/)  
    If the [IP is listed under the Spamhaus Blocklist (SBL)](https://check.spamhaus.org/sbl/listings/ovh.net/), this means our abuse reporting team has already contacted you by email.

      The email sent by our team comes from an address similar to this one: `ticket+ABCDEFGHIJ.ID@abuse.ovh.net` (the ticket number is always in capital letters and the ID that follows is composed of 4 alphanumeric characters).

      Follow the instructions in this email to resolve the situation. Once you have taken corrective measures, we will forward them to Spamhaus and request that your IP be removed from the list. Spamhaus remains the sole decision-maker regarding our request.
    - [Exploits Block List (XBL)](https://www.spamhaus.org/blocklists/exploits-blocklist/) or [Combined Spam Sources (CSS)](https://www.spamhaus.org/blocklists/combined-spam-sources/)  
    If your IP is listed under Exploits Block List and/or Combined Spam Sources, this is usually due to configuration issues. Follow the steps described on the Spamhaus website to delist the IP (see the example below). Once you have followed them, you can delist the IP yourself.

- [SpamCop](https://www.spamcop.net/bl.shtml)  
    On this page, you can check the current status of an IP. The SpamCop Blocking List (SCBL) is time-based: an IP is delisted automatically once spam reports stop, so there is usually no manual removal request to submit.

- [Barracuda](https://www.barracudacentral.org/lookups)  
    To request removal from the Barracuda Reputation Block List (BRBL), submit the [Barracuda removal request form](https://www.barracudacentral.org/rbl/removal-request). Listings are manually verified, so removal is not immediate.

- [SpamRATS](https://spamrats.com/lookup.php)  
    If you are running your own email server, you must configure the domain name in the PTR record, where the contact information for the responsible party can be found. Only properly configured email servers are removed from this list.  
    You must also [configure the reverse DNS resolution](/guides/bare-metal-cloud/virtual-private-servers/configuring-reverse-dns).
    
    :::info
    **Best practices:**
    
    IP addresses used to send email should resolve to the responsible party's domain name. You can also use subdomains for the reverse DNS resolution, such as `mail.domain_name.com` or `gateway.domain_name.com`.

    :::



### Unsupported providers

#### s5h.net

<details>

<summary>More information</summary>


To request removal, visit the [usenix RBL removal page](https://www.usenix.org.uk/content/rblremove) from the IP address that has been blocklisted. The IP should be delisted immediately.

You can also do this with the tools *telnet*, *curl* or *wget*.

To delist your IPv4 address via *curl*, log in to the listed mail server and run:

```bash
curl -4 http://www.usenix.org.uk/content/rblremove
```

To do the same using *telnet* from a Windows/Linux host, enter the *GET* and *Host* lines after the *telnet* command as shown below.

```bash
telnet www.usenix.org.uk 80
```

```bash
GET /content/rblremove HTTP/1.1
```

```bash
Host: www.usenix.org.uk
```

For a detailed explanation, see the [usenix RBL documentation](https://www.usenix.org.uk/content/rbl.html).

</details>


#### UCEprotect


<details>

<summary>More information</summary>


UCEprotect periodically places large numbers of ASNs on its level-3 blocklist, and the OVHcloud ASN (AS16276) may be affected. To view the list of affected ASNs and the number of newly added ASNs, check the following links:

- [UCEPROTECT Level 3 charts](https://www.uceprotect.net/en/l3charts.php)
- [UCEPROTECT statistics centre](https://stats.uceprotect.net/?page=su)

Our Abuse team contacts UCEprotect to request the removal of our ASN from the blocklist. UCEprotect requires the network operators of the blocked ASNs to pay for express delisting. Like all major providers, OVHcloud does not pay for blocklist delisting, as it is a service outside of our control. Paying for delisting only increases blocklisting overall and ultimately harms the industry.

UCEprotect states that it automatically delists ASNs after one week. As this is outside of our control, we cannot provide any guarantee in this regard.

If you are currently affected, we recommend the following:

1. Use IPv6 addresses to send emails. UCEprotect does not blocklist email sent over IPv6. All OVHcloud services include at least one IPv6 address that you can configure, and all major email providers now support IPv6.
2. Ask the recipient to contact their email provider and request that they stop using the UCEprotect blocklist.


</details>


#### Fabel Spamsources

<details>

<summary>More information</summary>


To delist an IP from Fabel Spamsources, navigate to their [delisting page](https://www.spamsources.fabel.dk/delist).

Click <code className="action">Please login to continue</code>, enter your email address, and check your email inbox. Use the verification code to finish logging in.

Enter your IP address, provide a reason for the removal request, and click <code className="action">Submit Query</code>.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Fabel Spamsources delisting form with IP address and reason fields" src="/images/network/additional-ip/blocklist-ip-delist/blocklist3.png" loading="lazy" />

Delisting should take between 20 and 30 minutes to complete.

</details>



#### MIPSpace

<details>

<summary>More information</summary>


To [delist an IP from MIPSpace](https://www.mipspace.com/removal.php), first log in to the <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Control Panel</ManagerLink> and ensure that the following information is up-to-date:

- The details of your organisation (*RWhois*). Navigate to <code className="action">Network</code> > <code className="action">Public network</code> > <code className="action">Public IP Addresses</code>, then open the <code className="action">Manage my organisations</code> tab.

</details>
