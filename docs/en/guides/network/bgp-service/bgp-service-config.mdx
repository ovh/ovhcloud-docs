## Objective

BGP Service allows you to build highly available infrastructures by running standard BGP (Border Gateway Protocol) straight from your OVHcloud hosts. Your hosts establish private BGP sessions with OVHcloud routers over your vRack and announce your public IP blocks, either OVHcloud Additional IPs or your own IP addresses imported with Bring Your Own IP (BYOIP).

BGP Service is an option of [vRack Services](/guides/network/vrack-services/global): you order it, configure it and manage it from the **BGP Service** tab of your vRack Services.

**This guide explains how to order and configure BGP Service, and how to set up the BGP sessions on your hosts.**

:::info
BGP Service is currently in Beta and is free of charge during this phase. Features and limits may change before general availability.
:::

## Requirements

- An OVHcloud customer account with access to the <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Control Panel</ManagerLink>.
- A [vRack private network](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/network/vrack/) and a [vRack Services](/guides/network/vrack-services/global) in one of the [available regions](#available-regions). If your vRack Services is not yet linked to a vRack, you can select or create one while ordering BGP Service.
- The servers or instances that will take part in the BGP peering (Bare Metal servers, Hosted Private Cloud VMware on OVHcloud virtual machines or Public Cloud instances), connected to the vRack and directly connected to the subnet you will use for BGP.
- At least one public IP block to announce: an [Additional IP](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/network/additional-ip/) block, or your own IP block imported with [Bring Your Own IP (BYOIP)](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/network/byoip/).
- Knowledge of IP networking, of the BGP routing protocol and of Linux network settings.

## How BGP Service works

BGP Service adds Layer 3 routing on top of your vRack Services:

- **BGP Service**: one per vRack Services, therefore one per region for a given vRack. It holds the ASNs used on both sides of the BGP sessions and the list of public IP blocks you are allowed to announce.
- **BGP range**: a small block of private addresses (a /28 for IPv4, a /124 for IPv6) carved from a subnet of your vRack Services. It contains the peering IPs used on both ends of the BGP sessions: 4 OVHcloud peering IPs and up to 4 customer peering IPs. You configure one BGP range for IPv4 and, optionally, one for IPv6.
- **Public IP blocks**: the Additional IP or BYOIP blocks that OVHcloud accepts from your BGP sessions. A block that is not on this list cannot be announced.

Your hosts announce your public IP blocks to the OVHcloud peering IPs. OVHcloud propagates them to the internet and, in return, advertises a default route to your hosts. When the same block is announced by several hosts, incoming traffic is distributed between them (ECMP). If a host stops announcing the block, its traffic is rerouted to the remaining hosts, without any call to the OVHcloud API.

## Service capabilities and limits

Before setting up BGP Service, be aware of the following capabilities and constraints:

- **One BGP Service per vRack Services**: a vRack Services is regional, and a vRack can be linked to only one vRack Services per region.
- **Regions**: BGP Service is available in 1-AZ regions only (see [Available regions](#available-regions)). 3-AZ, US and APAC regions are not supported yet.
- **BGP subnet**: one IPv4 subnet and one IPv6 subnet of your vRack Services can be used for BGP. These subnets must be untagged (no VLAN), and the subnet used by a configuration cannot be changed afterwards.
- **BGP range size**: /28 for IPv4, /124 for IPv6.
- **Peering IPs**: per IP version, exactly 4 OVHcloud peering IPs and 1 to 4 customer peering IPs, all within the BGP range. If you need more than 4 BGP peering hosts, deploy Route Servers (see the [Advanced BGP configuration](#use-case-advanced-bgp-configuration-using-route-servers-rs) use case).
- **Hosts**: up to 10 hosts in total, including the hosts behind Route Servers. Up to 4 Route Servers.
- **ASNs**: private ASNs only. The customer ASN must be between 64512 and 65533 (64512 by default), the OVHcloud ASN between 64513 and 65534 (65534 by default), and the two values must be different.
- **Public IP blocks**: up to 50 blocks per IP version. Each BGP peer can announce up to 32 prefixes per IP version.
- **Public IP block sizes**:

| Source | IPv4 | IPv6 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Additional IP | /24 to /30 | /56 |
| BYOIP | /19 to /24 | /56 (a larger imported block must be split into /56 blocks) |

- **Announcement sizes**: you can announce a whole public IP block or any subset of it, down to /32 for IPv4 and /64 for IPv6.
- **Dedicated IP blocks**: a public IP block can be added to only one BGP Service at a time. Only Additional IP blocks that are not associated with any other service (Dedicated Server, Public Cloud instance, etc.) can be selected.
- **Routes received**: OVHcloud advertises a default route only.
- **Bandwidth**: 5,000 Mbps per BGP Service.
- **Not supported**: MD5 authentication of the BGP sessions, BGP communities, public ASNs and anycast announcements.
- **BFD support**: Bidirectional Forwarding Detection (BFD) is enabled by default on the OVHcloud side, with fixed settings (500 ms interval, 8x multiplier). To use BFD, configure your hosts to match these values.
- **Monitoring**: the BGP Service tab shows the status of the service. The state of each BGP session is not displayed.

## Potential use cases

The BGP Service supports a wide range of custom networking architectures; the following examples highlight some of the most frequent implementations:

- **Run your own routing stack** (FRR/Bird/VMware NSX, etc.) on Bare Metal or virtual routers and establish standard BGP sessions directly with OVHcloud Edges.
- **Build high‑availability frontends**: use BGP to move IPs between multiple servers (active/active or active/passive) by changing BGP announcements, enabling fast failover actions.
- **Set up ECMP / multi-path routing** between your hosts and the OVHcloud backbone to increase resilience across multiple servers or VMs.
- **Replace the Additional IP failover mechanism**: keep the same Additional IP block, but announce it with BGP to fail over in seconds and serve traffic from several hosts at once.
- **Design multi-tenant service nodes** by replacing L2 proxy-ARP with native L3 routing, where customer IP prefixes are routed through your infrastructure as the next hop.

## Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare your public IP blocks

You need to either buy an [Additional IP](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/network/additional-ip/) block from OVHcloud or import your own IP block with BYOIP. To import your IPs, follow [our BYOIP documentation](/guides/network/bring-your-own-ip/bring-your-own-ip).

The block must be in the same region as your vRack Services.

:::warning
An Additional IP block used with BGP Service **must not** be associated with any service (for example a Bare Metal server). Leave it unassigned (in parking): only unassigned blocks are offered when you configure BGP Service.
:::

### Step 2: Prepare your vRack and vRack Services

BGP Service runs on a vRack Services, in the region where your hosts are located. If you do not have one yet, create a vRack Services in this region and link it to the vRack that contains your hosts (see our [vRack Services guide](/guides/network/vrack-services/global)).

:::info
The public IP block does **not** need to be attached to the vRack: it is announced through the BGP sessions, not through the vRack.
:::

### Step 3: Order BGP Service

[[cpnav:network-vrack-services]]

Select your vRack Services, open the <code className="action">BGP Service</code> tab, then click <code className="action">Order BGP Service</code>.

On the **Order your BGP Service** page:

- If your vRack Services is not yet linked to a vRack, select the vRack to use. Only the vRacks that are not already linked to another vRack Services in the same region are offered. To use a new vRack, click <code className="action">Create a new vRack</code>: once the vRack is created, it is selected automatically.
- Check the order summary, then click <code className="action">Continue my order</code> and complete your order.

Once your BGP Service is delivered, the **BGP Service** tab shows its information and its status, **Draft**, until you configure it.

### Step 4: Configure IPv4 for BGP Service

In the <code className="action">BGP Service</code> tab, open <code className="action">IPv4 Configuration</code> and click <code className="action">Configure IPv4</code>.

:::info
IPv4 must be configured first: the IPv6 configuration becomes available once IPv4 is configured.
:::

Fill in the form:

| Field | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Subnet** | The vRack Services subnet in which the BGP sessions run. Only untagged subnets can be used. If your vRack Services has no suitable subnet, click <code className="action">Create a subnet</code> and enter a **Subnet name** (optional) and an **IP address range (CIDR)**. The subnet cannot be changed once the configuration is saved. |
| **BGP range** | A /28 block within the selected subnet, for example `10.35.0.16/28`. It must not overlap the service range used by your Service Endpoints in this subnet. |
| **Customer private ASN** | The ASN of your hosts, between 64512 and 65533. If you leave the field empty, 64512 is used. |
| **Customer Peering IPs** | 1 to 4 IP addresses within the BGP range, comma-separated. These are the private IPs of your hosts (or Route Servers) that establish the BGP sessions. |
| **OVHcloud ASN** | The ASN of the OVHcloud routers, between 64513 and 65534. If you leave the field empty, 65534 is used. It must be different from your ASN. |
| **OVHcloud Peering IPs** | Exactly 4 IP addresses within the BGP range, comma-separated. These are the private IPs of the OVHcloud routers. |
| **Public IP blocks** | The IPv4 blocks you want to announce. The list shows your unassigned IP blocks located in the same region as your vRack Services. |

Once you have entered a valid BGP range, the form suggests peering IPs (shown in grey) derived from it. For example, with the BGP range `10.35.0.16/28`:

| Addresses | Usage |
| :--- | :--- |
| 10.35.0.16 | Network address (reserved) |
| 10.35.0.17 to 10.35.0.20 | OVHcloud Peering IPs |
| 10.35.0.21 to 10.35.0.24 | Customer Peering IPs |
| 10.35.0.25 to 10.35.0.30 | Unused |
| 10.35.0.31 | Broadcast address (reserved) |

Click <code className="action">Save</code>.

Once the configuration is applied, the **Service Status** of your BGP Service changes to **Ready**. If it remains **Draft**, hover over the status to see what is missing: **Missing BGP Range** or **Missing Allowed Prefix** (no public IP block selected).

### Step 5: Configure IPv6 for BGP Service (optional)

To announce IPv6 blocks, open <code className="action">IPv6 Configuration</code> in the <code className="action">BGP Service</code> tab and click <code className="action">Configure IPv6</code>.

The form is the same as for IPv4, with the following differences:

- The subnet is an IPv6 subnet with private ULA addresses (`fd00::/8`), for example `fd00::/64`.
- The BGP range is a /124 block within this subnet, for example `fd00::10/124`.
- The public IP blocks are IPv6 /56 blocks.

Click <code className="action">Save</code>.

### Step 6: Configure BGP on your hosts

You can now set up the BGP sessions on your side, using the values displayed in the **Configuration** section of the <code className="action">BGP Service</code> tab:

| Value in the BGP Service tab | Parameter in the configuration examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Customer private ASN** | `CUSTOMER_ASN` |
| **OVHcloud private ASN** | `OVHcloud_ASN` |
| **Customer Peering IPs** | `HOST_IPV4` / `HOST_IPV6` (or `RS_IPV4` / `RS_IPV6` for Route Servers) |
| **OVHcloud Peering IPs** | `EDGE_IPV4` / `EDGE_IPV6` |
| **Public IP blocks** | `CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4` / `CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6` |

Each customer peering IP must be configured on a host (or Route Server) directly connected to the BGP subnet.

The following use cases walk you through two typical setups: load balancing with up to 4 hosts, and a setup with Route Servers for up to 10 hosts.

:::info
OVHcloud is not responsible for the configuration of the BGP daemon on your hosts. It is your responsibility to configure the BGP daemon on your hosts. The configurations below are examples for your consideration.
:::

## Use case: Basic BGP configuration - load balancing using BGP ECMP

This design is suitable for setups with **up to 4 BGP peering hosts**, as the number of customer peering IPs is limited to 4. If you need more than 4 hosts, use the [Advanced BGP configuration using Route Servers](#use-case-advanced-bgp-configuration-using-route-servers-rs) use case described below.

Below is a simple architecture that allows you to perform load balancing of your traffic on 3 hosts:

<img className="thumbnail" alt="BGPaaS Basic Architecture" src="/images/network/bgp-service/bgp-service-config/bgpaas_basic-peering.png" loading="lazy" />

To achieve this setup, you need to install a BGP daemon, like FRR, on each host, and configure it.

### Configuration parameters

The following parameters are to be substituted in your router configuration files with the values of your BGP Service configuration.

| Parameter | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **OVHcloud_ASN** | Private ASN used on OVHcloud Edges (**OVHcloud private ASN**) |
| **CUSTOMER_ASN** | Private ASN used on your hosts (**Customer private ASN**) |
| **CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4 <br/> CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6** | Public prefixes allocated for IPv4 and IPv6 usages (**Public IP blocks**) |
| **EDGE_IPV4 <br/> EDGE_IPV6** | OVHcloud Edges IP addresses in private/ULA range used for BGP peering and connectivity inside the vRack (**OVHcloud Peering IPs**) |
| **HOST_IPV4 <br/> HOST_IPV6** | Customer hosts IP addresses in private/ULA range used as BGP next hop and peer inside the vRack (**Customer Peering IPs**) |

### Configuring a BGP daemon (FRR)

To configure the BGP sessions using FRR, follow these next steps.

#### Step 1: Install FRR

On a Debian-based system, install FRR with:

```bash
sudo apt update && sudo apt install frr frr-pythontools
```

#### Step 2: Configure FRR

:::info
All of the following parameters should be present in the `/etc/frr/frr.conf` configuration file of the hosts.
:::

##### Prefix list and route map configuration

:::info
The configuration below is a suggested setup to prevent any unexpected announcement between BGP peers.
:::

In the following example:

- Hosts only accept default routes from OVHcloud Edges.
- Hosts advertise customer's prefixes to OVHcloud Edges.

Related prefix lists and route maps to filter routes:

```bash
ip prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4 seq 10 permit 0.0.0.0/0

ip prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4 seq 10 permit <CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4> ge <length>
... <other sequences may be added depending on the customer setup>

ipv6 prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6 seq 10 permit ::/0

ipv6 prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6 seq 10 permit <CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6> eq <length>
... <other sequences may be added depending on the customer setup>


route-map RM_EDGE_V4_OUT permit 10
  match ip address prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4
route-map RM_EDGE_V4_IN permit 10
  match ip address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4

route-map RM_EDGE_V6_OUT permit 10
  match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6
route-map RM_EDGE_V6_IN permit 10
  match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6
```

##### BFD configuration

:::info
The configuration below is a suggested setup to improve BGP convergence time between Host and Edges over vRack.
:::

```bash
bfd
 profile edge
  detect-multiplier 8
  receive-interval 500
  transmit-interval 500

 peer <EDGE_IPV4>
  profile edge
  no shutdown
...
 peer <EDGE_IPV6>
  profile edge
  no shutdown
...
```

##### BGP configuration

Global configuration:

```bash
router bgp <CUSTOMER_ASN>
 bgp router-id <HOST_IPV4>
 no bgp default ipv4-unicast
 maximum-paths 16
 maximum-paths ibgp 16
```

BGP peering with OVHcloud Edge:

```bash
router bgp <CUSTOMER_ASN>
 neighbor PG_EDGE_V4 peer-group
 neighbor PG_EDGE_V4 remote-as <OVHcloud_ASN>
 neighbor PG_EDGE_V4 bfd
 neighbor PG_EDGE_V6 peer-group
 neighbor PG_EDGE_V6 remote-as <OVHcloud_ASN>
 neighbor PG_EDGE_V6 bfd
 neighbor <EDGE_IPV4> peer-group PG_EDGE_V4
...
 neighbor <EDGE_IPV6> peer-group PG_EDGE_V6
...

 address-family ipv4 unicast
  neighbor PG_EDGE_V4 activate
  neighbor PG_EDGE_V4 route-map RM_EDGE_V4_IN in
  neighbor PG_EDGE_V4 route-map RM_EDGE_V4_OUT out
 address-family ipv6 unicast
  neighbor PG_EDGE_V6 activate
  neighbor PG_EDGE_V6 route-map RM_EDGE_V6_IN in
  neighbor PG_EDGE_V6 route-map RM_EDGE_V6_OUT out
```

#### Step 3: Restart FRR

After editing the configuration, restart FRR to apply changes:

```bash
sudo systemctl restart frr
```

#### Step 4: Verify BGP session

Check the status of your BGP sessions with:

```bash
vtysh -c 'show bgp summary'
```

The sessions with the OVHcloud Edges must be in the `Established` state.

#### Step 5: Verify ingress and egress connectivity

To ensure your BGP session is functioning correctly, test both inbound and outbound traffic:

- **Check ingress traffic**

Use a remote server to ping or traceroute to your advertised IP prefix:

```bash
ping YOUR_ADVERTISED_IP
traceroute YOUR_ADVERTISED_IP
```

Verify that traffic reaches your network via the expected BGP paths.

- **Check egress traffic**

From your server, check the routing table and ensure your BGP routes are in use:

```bash
ip route show
vtysh -c 'show ip route bgp'
```

Confirm that outbound traffic is following the correct BGP paths.

## Use case: Advanced BGP configuration using Route Servers (RS)

If you want to use more than 4 hosts with BGP Service, you need to deploy and manage Route Servers (RS). This design allows you to scale **up to 10 hosts/next hops**. Each Route Server uses one of the 4 customer peering IPs, so you can deploy up to 4 Route Servers. The Route Server can be deployed either in-path or out-of-path, depending on your architecture requirements.

An RS peers with Edges and Hosts, establishing two sessions per peer (one for IPv4 and one for IPv6).

And here is a detailed view of the BGP sessions between Edges, RS and Hosts:

<img className="thumbnail" alt="BGPaaS sessions detail" src="/images/network/bgp-service/bgp-service-config/shadow_bgpaas_rs-peering.png" loading="lazy" />

To achieve this setup, you need to install a BGP daemon, like FRR, on each host, and configure it.

### Configuration parameters

The following parameters are to be substituted in your router configuration files with the values of your BGP Service configuration.

| Parameter | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **OVHcloud_ASN** | Private ASN used on OVHcloud Edges (**OVHcloud private ASN**) |
| **CUSTOMER_ASN** | Private ASN used on your Route Servers and hosts (**Customer private ASN**) |
| **CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4 <br/> CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6** | Public prefixes allocated for IPv4 and IPv6 usages (**Public IP blocks**) |
| **RS_IPV4 <br/> RS_IPV6** | Customer RS IP addresses in private/ULA range used for BGP peering and connectivity inside the vRack (**Customer Peering IPs**) |
| **EDGE_IPV4 <br/> EDGE_IPV6** | OVHcloud Edges IP addresses in private/ULA range used for BGP peering and connectivity inside the vRack (**OVHcloud Peering IPs**) |
| **HOST_IPV4 <br/> HOST_IPV6** | Other customer hosts IP addresses in private/ULA range used as BGP next hop and peer inside the vRack. They must belong to the BGP subnet. |

### Configuring a BGP daemon (FRR)

To configure the BGP sessions using FRR, follow the steps below.

#### Step 1: Install FRR

On a Debian-based system, install FRR with:

```bash
sudo apt update && sudo apt install frr frr-pythontools
```

#### Step 2: Configure FRR

##### ***FRR configuration for Route Servers***

:::info
All of the following parameters should be present in the `/etc/frr/frr.conf` configuration file of the route server(s).
:::

##### Prefix list and route map configuration

:::info
The configuration below is a suggested setup to prevent any unexpected announcement between BGP peers.
:::

Route Servers accept default routes from OVHcloud Edges and all routes from Hosts if they match the defined prefix length (see the OVHcloud rules for IPv4 and IPv6 prefix length).
Route Servers advertise Hosts routes to OVHcloud Edges and Default routes to Hosts.

Related prefix lists and route maps to filter routes:

```bash
ip prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4 seq 10 permit 0.0.0.0/0

ip prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4 seq 10 permit <CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4> ge <length>
... <other sequences may be added depending on the customer setup>

ipv6 prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6 seq 10 permit ::/0

ipv6 prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6 seq 10 permit <CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6> eq <length>
... <other sequences may be added depending on the customer setup>


route-map RM_EDGE_V4_OUT deny 10
 match ip address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4
route-map RM_EDGE_V4_OUT permit 20
  match ip address prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4

route-map RM_EDGE_V4_IN permit 10
 match ip address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4

route-map RM_HOST_V4_IN deny 10
  match ip address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4
route-map RM_HOST_V4_IN permit 20
  match ip address prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V4

route-map RM_HOST_V4_OUT permit 10
 match ip address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V4


route-map RM_EDGE_V6_OUT deny 10
 match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6
route-map RM_EDGE_V6_OUT permit 20
  match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6

route-map RM_EDGE_V6_IN permit 10
 match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6

route-map RM_HOST_V6_IN deny 10
  match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6
route-map RM_HOST_V6_IN permit 20
  match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_CUSTOMER_PREFIX_V6

route-map RM_HOST_V6_OUT permit 10
 match ipv6 address prefix-list PL_DEFAULT_ROUTE_V6
```

The configuration below is a suggested setup to improve BGP convergence time between Hosts and RS over vRack.
:::

The values below are just an example, and you may choose other values for your RS and Hosts.

```bash
bfd
 profile rs
  detect-multiplier 8
  receive-interval 500
  transmit-interval 500

 peer <RS_IPV4>
  profile rs
  no shutdown
...
 peer <RS_IPV6>
  profile rs
  no shutdown
...
```

##### BGP configuration

Global configuration:

```bash
router bgp <CUSTOMER_ASN>
 bgp router-id <HOST_IPV4>
 no bgp default ipv4-unicast
 maximum-paths 16
 maximum-paths ibgp 16
```

BGP peering with RS:

```bash
router bgp <CUSTOMER_ASN>
 neighbor PG_RS_V4 peer-group
 neighbor PG_RS_V4 remote-as <CUSTOMER_ASN>
 neighbor PG_RS_V4 bfd
 neighbor PG_RS_V6 peer-group
 neighbor PG_RS_V6 remote-as <CUSTOMER_ASN>
 neighbor PG_RS_V6 bfd
 neighbor <RS_IPV4> peer-group PG_RS_V4
...
 neighbor <RS_IPV6> peer-group PG_RS_V6
...

 address-family ipv4 unicast
  neighbor PG_RS_V4 activate
  neighbor PG_RS_V4 route-map RM_RS_V4_IN in
  neighbor PG_RS_V4 route-map RM_RS_V4_OUT out
 address-family ipv6 unicast
  neighbor PG_RS_V6 activate
  neighbor PG_RS_V6 route-map RM_RS_V6_IN in
  neighbor PG_RS_V6 route-map RM_RS_V6_OUT out
```

#### Step 3: Restart FRR

On each host/RS, after editing the configuration, restart FRR to apply changes:

```bash
sudo systemctl restart frr
```

#### Step 4: Verify BGP session

Check the status of your BGP sessions on your different hosts/RS with:

```bash
vtysh -c 'show bgp summary'
```

The sessions must be in the `Established` state.

#### Step 5: Verify ingress and egress connectivity

To ensure your BGP session is functioning correctly, test both inbound and outbound traffic:

- **Check ingress traffic**

Use a remote server to ping or traceroute to your advertised IP prefix:

```bash
ping YOUR_ADVERTISED_IP
traceroute YOUR_ADVERTISED_IP
```

Verify that traffic reaches your network via the expected BGP paths.

- **Check egress traffic**

From your server, check the routing table and ensure your BGP routes are in use:

```bash
ip route show
vtysh -c 'show ip route bgp'
```

Confirm that outbound traffic is following the correct BGP paths.

## Manage your BGP Service

All the actions below are available in the <code className="action">BGP Service</code> tab of your vRack Services.

### Modify a configuration

In the **Configuration** section, select <code className="action">IPv4 Configuration</code> or <code className="action">IPv6 Configuration</code>, then click <code className="action">Modify IPv4 configuration</code> (or <code className="action">Modify IPv6 configuration</code>).

You can change the BGP range, the ASNs, the peering IPs and the public IP blocks. To remove a public IP block, click the cross on its label. The subnet cannot be changed.

Some changes restart the BGP sessions and may interrupt your traffic:

| Change | Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| Change the customer or OVHcloud peering IPs | No interruption |
| Add or remove a public IP block (at least one block remains) | No interruption |
| Change the customer or OVHcloud ASN | The BGP sessions restart |
| Change the BGP range | The BGP sessions restart |
| Remove the last public IP block | The announcement stops and the status returns to **Draft** |

When one of these changes is pending, a banner warns you that the service will restart and lists the affected fields. Plan these changes during a maintenance window, then click <code className="action">Save</code>.

### Rename your BGP Service

In the **Information** section, click the pencil icon next to the **BGP Service Name**, enter the new name, then save.

### Terminate your BGP Service

In the **Information** section, click <code className="action">Terminate BGP service</code>, then confirm with <code className="action">Terminate</code>.

Your BGP sessions and prefix announcements are terminated, and the service stops at the end of the billing period. This action cannot be undone. Before terminating, make sure your traffic no longer depends on the announced IP blocks.

## Best practices for production

### Maintenance of a host without traffic interruption

To offload a server for maintenance (e.g. OS update, hardware intervention) without traffic interruption, you can use `BGP graceful shutdown` (RFC 8326). This mechanism signals peers to deprioritize routes toward the host *before* the session goes down, allowing traffic to drain to the remaining hosts without packet loss. This **does not mean that sessions (such as TCP) are maintained** if they are not synchronized between hosts announcing the route.

With FRR, you can initiate a `graceful shutdown` on the host to be maintained:

```bash
vtysh -c 'configure terminal' -c 'router bgp <CUSTOMER_ASN>' -c 'bgp graceful-shutdown'
```

This causes FRR to tag all advertised routes with the `GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN` community, signalling peers to prefer alternative paths. Once traffic has drained (verify by checking route tables on the other hosts or the Route Server), you can safely proceed with maintenance.

To restore the host after maintenance, disable `graceful shutdown`:

```bash
vtysh -c 'configure terminal' -c 'router bgp <CUSTOMER_ASN>' -c 'no bgp graceful-shutdown'
```

The host will resume advertising routes normally and start receiving traffic again.

## Troubleshooting

If you encounter issues with your BGP Service:

- **The status remains Draft**: hover over the status to see the reason. **Missing BGP Range** means the configuration is incomplete; **Missing Allowed Prefix** means that no public IP block is selected.
- **A public IP block is not offered in the list**: check that the block is in the same region as your vRack Services, that it is not associated with another service and that it is not already used by another BGP Service.
- **A BGP session does not reach the `Established` state**: check that the host uses one of the customer peering IPs, that it is directly connected to the BGP subnet, and that the ASNs configured on the host match the **Customer private ASN** and **OVHcloud private ASN** values.
- **Your prefix is not reachable from the internet**: check that it belongs to one of your public IP blocks, that its size is allowed, and that there are no conflicting announcements.
- Ensure your firewall and network policies allow BGP traffic (TCP port 179) and, if you use it, BFD.

If the issue persists, create a support request in our [Help Centre](/links/support-contact).
