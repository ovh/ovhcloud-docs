## Objective

You can add an [SSL certificate](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/web-hosting/options/ssl/) to your Content Delivery Network (CDN), so that your users can connect securely, even if they do so via the CDN.

**In this guide, we will show you how to do this with a Let's Encrypt certificate, delivered by OVHcloud, or with your own certificate.**

## Requirements

- an [OVHcloud CDN solution](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/network/cdn/).
- the permissions needed to manage your domain’s DNS zone

[[cpnav:network-cdn|en]]

## Instructions

### Get the Let’s Encrypt certificate supplied by OVHcloud for your subdomains using the CDN.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:lets-encrypt-certificate */}

- If your CDN does not have a certificate yet, you can have a free Let's Encrypt certificate created when you add a domain: in the **Guided domain setup**, choose **Let's Encrypt** at the **SSL certificate** step. You can also create it at any time from the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab: click <code className="action">Add certificate</code>, select **Let's Encrypt (free)**, enter a name for the certificate and click <code className="action">Add certificate</code>.
- If your CDN already has a certificate when you add another subdomain, the guided setup skips the SSL step: the certificate is automatically regenerated to include the new subdomain that you have just added.

To ensure that the certificate is generated properly, the subdomain you add needs to point correctly to the CDN, and port 80 must be reachable on the CDN for the Let's Encrypt validation. To check that your subdomain points to the CDN, follow the instructions in our guide to [Configuring a domain name for the first time](/guides/network/content-delivery-network-infrastructure/cdn-infrastructure-first-domain-configuration).

The certificate is renewed automatically 20 days before its expiry date.

:::warning
The Let's Encrypt certificate supplied by OVHcloud can include the first 100 domains or subdomains configured on the CDN. If you would like to do this for more than 100 domains, you will need to configure your own wildcard/multi-domain SSL certificate.
:::

{/* CP-STEPS-END:lets-encrypt-certificate */}

### How long does it take to generate a certificate?

{/* CP-STEPS-START:generation-time */}

Once your domain points correctly to the CDN, generating (or regenerating) a Let's Encrypt certificate and deploying it takes about 2 minutes.

While an SSL operation is in progress, the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab tells you so and refreshes automatically.

If the generation takes longer than expected, the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab displays a message and a <code className="action">View SSL operations</code> link, which lists the SSL operations of your CDN in the **Operations** tab. In this case, check that the domain name configured on your CDN points to the CDN and that port 80 is reachable. If this is not the case, our system will be unable to generate the certificate properly.

There is no timeout: as long as your DNS changes have not propagated, the generation waits, and it completes once the domain points to the CDN. You do not need to request a new certificate.

Once the certificate has been activated, the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab shows it under **Certificate information**: its name, its provider (**Let's Encrypt** or **Custom certificate**), its status, its common name (CN) and its validity dates.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:generation-time */}

### Add your own certificate.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:add-own-certificate */}

If your CDN does not have a certificate yet, you can import your own certificate from the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab, even before your DNS changes have propagated. Click <code className="action">Add certificate</code>, select **Import custom certificate**, then enter a name (optional), your certificate and its private key in PEM format and, if your certification authority requires it, the certificate chain. Click <code className="action">Add certificate</code> to confirm.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="SSL tab of a CDN without a certificate" src="/images/network/content-delivery-network-infrastructure/ssl-certificate/ssl-tab.png" loading="lazy" />

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Add certificate window" src="/images/network/content-delivery-network-infrastructure/ssl-certificate/ssl-add-certificate.png" loading="lazy" />

If your CDN already has a certificate, for example a Let's Encrypt certificate, click <code className="action">Update certificate</code> in the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab. Enter your new certificate, its private key and, optionally, its chain, then click <code className="action">Update certificate</code>.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:add-own-certificate */}
