## Objective

You manage the OVHcloud Load Balancer from a dedicated dashboard in the <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Console</ManagerLink>. The interface groups every part of the service into tabs and applies a staged configuration model: most changes are saved as pending edits and take effect only once you apply them.

This guide tours the management interface, describes the purpose of each tab, and explains the apply-configuration (refresh) model that activates your changes.

**In this guide, you will learn how to:**

- Locate and open your Load Balancer in the OVHcloud Console.
- Identify the role of each tab: Overview, Frontends, Server farms, SSL, Network, Logs, Statistics, Operations, and Quotas.
- Understand how pending changes are staged and applied.

## Requirements

- An [OVHcloud Load Balancer](/links/network/load-balancer) in your account.
- Access to the <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Console</ManagerLink>.

## Introduction

The OVHcloud Load Balancer distributes incoming traffic across multiple backend servers to improve availability and scale. It runs as a managed service: OVHcloud operates the underlying infrastructure, and you define the configuration (server farms, frontends, certificates, and routing) through the OVHcloud Console or the OVHcloud API. For a conceptual overview, see What is OVHcloud Load Balancer{/* TODO(iplb-revamp): restore this link before the final merge, once the revamp guide lands → [What is OVHcloud Load Balancer](/guides/network/load-balancer/what-is-load-balancer) */}.

### Open your Load Balancer <a name="open"></a>

{/* CP-STEPS-START:open-load-balancer */}
To reach the management dashboard for an existing Load Balancer:

1. Log in to the <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Console</ManagerLink>.
2. In the top bar, select <code className="action">Bare Metal Cloud</code> (or <code className="action">Private Cloud</code>). In the left menu, open <code className="action">Network</code> and select <code className="action">Load Balancer</code>.
3. Select your service from the list to open its dashboard.

The dashboard opens on the `Overview` tab. Its **General information** section summarises the service: ID, availability zones, vRack, deployment status, IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, and outbound IPv4 addresses. The service status is shown in the page header. The tabs across the top give access to each configurable area.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:open-load-balancer */}

To list your services through the OVHcloud API instead:

<Api version="v1" section="/ipLoadbalancing" method="GET" route="/ipLoadbalancing" />

To read the properties of a single service:

<Api version="v1" section="/ipLoadbalancing" method="GET" route={"/ipLoadbalancing/\{serviceName\}"} />

| Parameter | Required | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `serviceName` | Yes | The identifier of your Load Balancer service. | `loadbalancer-a1b2c3...` |

#### Server Farms

{/* CP-STEPS-START:server-farms */}
The `Server farms` tab lists every backend farm. A farm groups the servers that receive traffic and defines how that traffic is distributed. Click <code className="action">Create farm</code> to add a farm and <code className="action">Add server</code> to attach a backend to it.

Available load balancing algorithms are `Round Robin`, `Least Connections`, `Source Hash`, `URI Hash`, and `First Available`. Stickiness options are `None`, `Cookie`, and `Source IP`. These options are described in Configure stickiness{/* TODO(iplb-revamp): restore this link before the final merge, once the revamp guide lands → [Configure stickiness](/guides/network/load-balancer/configure-stickiness) */}.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:server-farms */}

#### Frontends

{/* CP-STEPS-START:frontends */}
The `Frontends` tab lists the listening endpoints. A frontend defines the protocol (`HTTP`, `HTTPS`, `TCP`, `TLS` or `UDP`), the listening port, and the availability zone. For an HTTP or HTTPS frontend, the **Default route** either sends traffic to a farm (**Route to farm**) or redirects it to a URL; a TCP frontend sends traffic to a **Default Farm** instead. Click <code className="action">Create frontend</code> to add one. For HTTPS frontends, attach a **TLS Certificate** from the `SSL` tab.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:frontends */}

#### SSL Certificates

{/* CP-STEPS-START:ssl-certificates */}
The `SSL` tab manages the certificates used for TLS termination. Click <code className="action">Add Certificate</code>, then choose the certificate type: `Custom (upload PEM)` to upload your own certificate, or `Free (Let's Encrypt)` for a free certificate. In the **SSL/TLS Configuration** section, set the **Cipher Profile** to `Modern (TLS 1.3)` or `Intermediate (TLS 1.2+)`. See Configure SSL certificates{/* TODO(iplb-revamp): restore this link before the final merge, once the revamp guide lands → [Configure SSL certificates](/guides/network/load-balancer/configure-ssl-certificates) */}.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:ssl-certificates */}

#### Subnet

{/* CP-STEPS-START:subnet */}
The `Network` tab groups two areas. The **vRack** section connects the Load Balancer to a vRack so that it can reach backends over a private network; use it when your servers are not reachable over a public address. Not every offer supports it: on an offer without vRack support, the section reads **vRack is not supported for this load balancer offer.**; otherwise it offers <code className="action">Attach a vRack</code>. The **Additional IPs** section lets you order Additional IPs, attach them to the Load Balancer and assign them to a frontend. See [Configure vRack](/guides/network/load-balancer/configure-vrack).
{/* CP-STEPS-END:subnet */}

#### Statistics

{/* CP-STEPS-START:statistics */}
The `Statistics` tab displays traffic graphs for the service: connections per second and requests per second, with their average and peak values, over a period you choose from one hour to one month. Use it to observe load patterns and confirm that traffic is reaching your farms.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:statistics */}

#### Logs

{/* CP-STEPS-START:logs */}
The `Logs` tab manages log forwarding. Click <code className="action">Manage your configuration</code> to list your log data streams, then click <code className="action">Subscribe</code> next to the stream that should receive the Load Balancer logs, for example a Logs Data Platform stream. The **Logs Explorer** on the same tab shows the logs live. See Configure logs forwarding{/* TODO(iplb-revamp): restore this link before the final merge, once the revamp guide lands → [Configure logs forwarding](/guides/network/load-balancer/configure-logs-forwarding) */}.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:logs */}

#### Tasks

{/* CP-STEPS-START:tasks */}
The `Operations` tab lists the operations the platform runs on your behalf when you change frontends, farms or SSL certificates, including each apply-configuration operation. Counters at the top give the number of operations per status (**Total**, **Done**, **Doing**, **Todo**, **Error**, **Blocked**, **Cancelled**), and the list refreshes itself every ten seconds while **Auto refresh (10s)** is enabled. Each operation shows its **ID**, **Action**, **Zone(s)**, **Status**, **Created on** and **Completion date**; click <code className="action">See details</code> to follow its progress. To confirm that an applied change has finished propagating, check that the **Deployment** row of the `Overview` tab reads **Up to date**.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:tasks */}

To list tasks through the OVHcloud API:

<Api version="v1" section="/ipLoadbalancing" method="GET" route={"/ipLoadbalancing/\{serviceName\}/task"} />

#### Quota

{/* CP-STEPS-START:quota */}
The `Quotas` tab shows the bandwidth your service has used during the current billing period, for each availability zone, as a percentage and as a volume against the zone's allowance. For each zone, you can set an **Alert threshold** to be warned before you reach that allowance. Review it before scaling a configuration.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:quota */}

To read quota information through the OVHcloud API:

<Api version="v1" section="/ipLoadbalancing" method="GET" route={"/ipLoadbalancing/\{serviceName\}/quota"} />

### The apply-configuration model <a name="apply"></a>

This section explains how the Load Balancer activates your changes. This model is essential to understand, because most edits do not take effect the moment you save them.

When you create or modify a farm, frontend, route, or certificate, the change is stored as a **pending change** rather than applied immediately. This lets you stage several edits and activate them together in a single, atomic operation. The active configuration keeps serving traffic with the previous settings until you apply.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The apply-configuration model diagram" src="/images/network/load-balancer/manage-lb-control-panel/fig-2.svg" loading="lazy" />

#### Review pending changes

Before applying, review what is staged so you activate only the changes you intend.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:review-pending-changes */}
<Tabs>
<Tab label="Console">

When changes are pending, the **Deployment** row of the **General information** section on the `Overview` tab shows how many there are (for example **2 pending changes**). The edits themselves are not listed there. Click <code className="action">Apply N changes</code>, where N is that number (for example <code className="action">Apply 2 changes</code>), to activate them.

</Tab>
<Tab label="OVHcloud API">

List the staged changes that have not yet been applied:

<Api version="v1" section="/ipLoadbalancing" method="GET" route={"/ipLoadbalancing/\{serviceName\}/pendingChanges"} />

| Parameter | Required | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `serviceName` | Yes | The identifier of your Load Balancer service. | `loadbalancer-a1b2c3...` |

</Tab>
</Tabs>
{/* CP-STEPS-END:review-pending-changes */}

#### Apply the configuration

Applying pushes every pending change to the active configuration in one operation.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:apply-configuration */}
<Tabs>
<Tab label="Console">

When changes are pending, the **Deployment** row of the **General information** section on the `Overview` tab offers a button named after their number, <code className="action">Apply N changes</code> (for example <code className="action">Apply 2 changes</code>). Click it to activate the staged configuration. No confirmation is requested: a message confirms that the deployment has started, and the **Deployment** row reads **Deployment in progress...** until it completes. The operation is listed in the `Operations` tab, where you can follow its progress.

</Tab>
<Tab label="OVHcloud API">

Apply all pending changes with a single call:

<Api version="v1" section="/ipLoadbalancing" method="POST" route={"/ipLoadbalancing/\{serviceName\}/refresh"} />

| Parameter | Required | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `serviceName` | Yes | The identifier of your Load Balancer service. | `loadbalancer-a1b2c3...` |

</Tab>
<Tab label="Terraform">

The OVHcloud Terraform provider can declare the staged objects — for example `ovh_iploadbalancing_http_farm`, `ovh_iploadbalancing_http_farm_server`, `ovh_iploadbalancing_http_route`, and `ovh_iploadbalancing_tcp_farm`. To apply the pending configuration from Terraform, use the `ovh_iploadbalancing_refresh` resource — or trigger the apply with the `POST /ipLoadbalancing/\{serviceName\}/refresh` API call, or with the <code className="action">Apply N changes</code> button in the OVHcloud Console, after Terraform has created or updated the resources.

</Tab>
</Tabs>
{/* CP-STEPS-END:apply-configuration */}

:::warning
Applying the configuration affects live traffic. Review the pending changes before you apply, and where possible schedule the operation during a maintenance window.
:::

:::tip
Stage related edits together — for example a new farm, its servers, and the frontend that points to it — then apply once. A single apply operation is atomic and avoids leaving the service in a half-configured state.
:::

#### Verify the change took effect

{/* CP-STEPS-START:verify-change */}
| Check | How | Expected result |
|---|---|---|
| Apply operation finished | Check the **Deployment** row of the `Overview` tab and the `Operations` tab, or call `GET /ipLoadbalancing/{serviceName}/task`. | **Deployment** reads **Up to date** and the apply operation shows the **Done** status; via the API, the most recent apply task shows a completed status. |
| No changes remain staged | Call `GET /ipLoadbalancing/{serviceName}/pendingChanges`. | The list is empty. |
| Service status healthy | Open the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab and check the status shown in the page header, or call `GET /ipLoadbalancing/{serviceName}/status`. | The status reflects the new configuration. |
{/* CP-STEPS-END:verify-change */}

### Limitations <a name="limitations"></a>

This section lists constraints that apply to the management interface and the apply model.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:limitations */}
| Limitation | Details |
|---|---|
| Changes are not live until applied | Edits to farms, frontends, routes, and certificates remain pending until you apply the configuration. |
| Apply is service-wide | An apply operation activates all pending changes at once; you cannot apply a single change in isolation. |
| Quota varies by offer and zone | The resources and bandwidth available per availability zone depend on your offer. The `Quotas` tab shows your bandwidth usage per zone for the current billing period. |
{/* CP-STEPS-END:limitations */}

### Troubleshooting <a name="troubleshooting"></a>

This section covers the most common issues you may encounter when managing the service from the OVHcloud Console.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:troubleshooting */}
| Issue | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| A saved change is not visible to clients | The configuration has not been applied. | Review pending changes, then apply the configuration. |
| The apply button is absent | There are no pending changes to apply: the **Deployment** row of the `Overview` tab reads **Up to date** and offers <code className="action">Redeploy the configuration</code> instead. | Make a configuration change first, then apply. |
| An apply operation is stuck or failed | A staged change conflicts with the current configuration. | Open the <code className="action">Operations</code> tab to check the operation, correct the conflicting change, and apply again. |
| A new farm or frontend cannot be created | A resource limit of your offer or zone has been reached. | Remove unused farms or frontends, or contact support to request a higher limit. The `Quotas` tab shows bandwidth usage only, not these limits. |
{/* CP-STEPS-END:troubleshooting */}
