## Objective

This guide is designed to help you get started using the Load Balancer, by introducing the main features of the solution.

## Requirements

- An [OVHcloud Load balancer](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/network/load-balancer/) service in your OVHcloud account.

[[cpnav:network-load-balancer]]

## Instructions

### Managing your Load Balancer in the OVHcloud Console

{/* CP-STEPS-START:manage-lb */}

In the OVHcloud Console, your Load Balancer service opens on the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab. On this page and the service's other tabs, you will see the following information:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|General information|On the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab: the ID of your Load Balancer, its zones, its vRack, the **Deployment** status of your configuration, your IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, and your **Outbound IPv4** addresses. The <code className="action">Add a zone</code> and <code className="action">Cancel zone</code> buttons manage the availability zones where your Load Balancer is based|
|Subscription|On the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab: the admin details for your solution (offer, creation date, renewal mode and date), with the <code className="action">Manage my subscription</code> and <code className="action">Terminate</code> buttons|
|Statistics|The <code className="action">Statistics</code> tab: the statistics of your service|
|Additional IPs|The Additional IPs linked to your Load Balancer are listed in the **Additional IPs** section of the <code className="action">Network</code> tab|
|Name and ciphers|You can customise the name of your solution with the pencil icon next to its name, at the top of the page. The cipher profile is customisable in the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab|

To add front-ends, click <code className="action">Create frontend</code> in the <code className="action">Frontends</code> tab. To add server farms, click <code className="action">Create farm</code> in the <code className="action">Server farms</code> tab. A page will then help you configure each part of your solution.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:manage-lb */}

### Managing front-ends

{/* CP-STEPS-START:manage-front-ends */}

To add front-ends, go to the <code className="action">Frontends</code> tab and click <code className="action">Create frontend</code>. The **Create Frontend** page then opens.

Details of front-end parts:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Name|If you want, you can choose a name for your front-end. This is very useful when you have several front-ends, so you can tell them apart quickly|
|Protocol|You can choose between HTTP, HTTPS, TCP, TLS and UDP|
|Port|Choose the listening port you wish to use: a single port (80), several ports (80,443) or a range (8080-8090)|
|Zone|Choose between your availability zone or all availability zones to create your front-end. The zone cannot be changed afterwards|
|Default Farm (TCP and UDP)|If you have several farms configured, you can choose one of them as a default for each front-end|
|Routing (HTTP and HTTPS)|Under **Default route**, choose **Route to farm** to select the default farm of the front-end, or **Redirect to URL** to add an HTTP redirection URL|

You can also access advanced settings, in the **Advanced settings** section:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Additional IPs|Additional IPs this front-end listens on. They must first be ordered and attached to the Load Balancer in the <code className="action">Network</code> tab. If none are selected, the front-end uses the main IP of the Load Balancer only|
|Whitelisted IPs|List of CIDR blocks for restricting remote access to the Load Balancer, for IPv4 only. Leave it empty to allow all|
|HTTP Header (HTTP and HTTPS)|Add a custom HTTP header to forward here|

{/* CP-STEPS-END:manage-front-ends */}

### Managing your clusters

{/* CP-STEPS-START:manage-clusters */}

To add a server farm, simply go to the <code className="action">Server farms</code> tab, and click <code className="action">Create farm</code>. The **Create Server Farm** page offers main options similar to those of the front-end: **Display name**, **Zone**, **Protocol** (HTTP, TCP or UDP) and **Port**. Its other options are in the **Load balancing** and **Health check** sections:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Algorithm|Choosing between First Available, Least Connections, Round Robin (default), Source Hash or URI Hash (HTTP only) for your IP load balancing|
|Stickiness|You can track sessions via Cookie (HTTP only) or source IP, or keep None|
|Health check|Tick **Enable health check probe** to activate a probe, then choose its **Probe type** (TCP, PGSQL, MYSQL, SMTP, OCO or HTTP), its **Port** and its **Interval (s)** (at least 30 seconds)|

The **Load balancing** and **Health check** sections are not available for a UDP farm.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:manage-clusters */}

### Managing your servers

{/* CP-STEPS-START:manage-servers */}
Once you have created your server cluster, you just need to add servers to it. Details on these options are below, as well as advanced options:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Name|If you want, you can choose a name for your server. This is very useful when you have several servers, so you can tell them apart quickly|
|IPv4 address|Adding the IP address of the service that will act as a server|
|Port|Server port|
|Backup server|Selecting which server is a backup server|

|PROXY Protocol version|**Only if you have configured it**, choose the version of the PROXY protocol you wish to use on the server. <br/> You can find more information on the PROXY protocol and how to use it with the OVHcloud Load Balancer in [this guide](/guides/network/load-balancer/create-proxyprotocol).|

### Managing SSL certificates

{/* CP-STEPS-START:manage-ssl-certificates */}

You can add an SSL certificate to the Load Balancer in the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab, in the **TLS Certificates** section, with the <code className="action">Add Certificate</code> button. You have two options: you can get a free Let's Encrypt certificate, or add an external certificate.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:manage-ssl-certificates */}

#### OVHcloud SSL certificate

{/* CP-STEPS-START:order-ssl-certificate */}

To get a free SSL certificate, simply go to the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab, click <code className="action">Add Certificate</code> and select **Free (Let's Encrypt)** as the **Certificate type**:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Certificate type|**Free (Let's Encrypt)**: an automatic free certificate, renewed every 90 days|
|Domain(s)|Domain(s) concerned (Fully Qualified Domain Names). Add the domain names one at a time|

{/* CP-STEPS-END:order-ssl-certificate */}

#### Adding an external SSL certificate

{/* CP-STEPS-START:add-external-ssl-certificate */}

If you already have your own SSL certificate, you can add it directly: in the <code className="action">SSL</code> tab, click <code className="action">Add Certificate</code> and select **Custom (upload PEM)** as the **Certificate type**:

|Element|Purpose|
|---|---|
|Display name|If you want, you can choose a name for your certificate. This is very useful when you have several certificates, so you can tell them apart quickly|
|Private Key (PEM)|Field for entering the private key to add to the service (required)|
|Certificate (PEM)|Field for adding the certificate (required)|
|Certificate Chain (PEM)|Field for adding the root certificate, if required|

Then click <code className="action">Add Certificate</code>.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:add-external-ssl-certificate */}
