## Objective

Monitoring lets you verify that your link and BGP session are healthy, follow their activity over time, get notified of key events, and pull point-in-time diagnostics. Use it to confirm a new setup is working as expected, and to gather evidence during an incident. This guide covers the tools and metrics available.

## Monitoring tools

### OVHcloud Console

{/* CP-STEPS-START:ovhcloud-control-panel */}
The OVHcloud Console gives you visibility into the following information:

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| **Link status** (**PoP Configuration** table, <code className="action">Configure</code> tab) | Whether the physical link is up or down. Displayed for OVHcloud Connect Direct services only. |
| **Optical statuses** (**PoP Configuration** table, <code className="action">Configure</code> tab) | IN/OUT optical light levels on the physical port (checked every 5 minutes). Displayed for OVHcloud Connect Direct services only. |
| **BGP status** (**PoP Configuration** table, <code className="action">Configure</code> tab) | Whether the BGP session is up or down. Displayed when at least one port is configured in Layer 3. |
| **Settings** section (<code className="action">Overview</code> tab) | Point of Presence, bandwidth, vRack, sector, circuit, number of ports (Direct), and service key (Provider). The state of the service is shown in the services list. |

BGP session state and learned/advertised routes are retrieved through the on-demand diagnostics described below.

The <code className="action">Statistics</code> tab of the service charts its activity over time. From the **Type** drop-down list, select **Network Traffic**, **Optical levels**, **Incoming BGP routes** or **Error rate** (optical levels and error rate are available for OVHcloud Connect Direct services only), and from the **Periodicity** drop-down list, select **Hourly**, **Daily** or **Weekly**.

To manage the notifications of a service, click the bell button on its row in the services list to open the **Notification management** window. It lets you enable or disable four notifications: **PoP Interface status** (interface up or down), **BGP status change**, **Optical level degradation** and **Packet loss**. The **Active notifications** column of the list shows how many notifications are enabled on each service.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:ovhcloud-control-panel */}

### OVHcloud API

For programmatic monitoring and integration with your own tools:

```python
import ovh

client = ovh.Client(endpoint='ovh-eu')

service_name = "your-occ-service-uuid"

# Get service status
service = client.get(f"/ovhCloudConnect/{service_name}")
print(f"Status: {service['status']}")

# List PoP configuration IDs, then fetch each PoP's status
pop_ids = client.get(f"/ovhCloudConnect/{service_name}/config/pop")
for pop_id in pop_ids:
    print(client.get(f"/ovhCloudConnect/{service_name}/config/pop/{pop_id}/status"))
```

> Full API reference: [eu.api.ovh.com/console](https://eu.api.ovh.com/console/?section=%2FovhCloudConnect&branch=v1)

### Your own monitoring tools

Beyond the statistics and notifications available in the OVHcloud Console, continuous monitoring of your side of the connection is done with your own tools. On your edge router and monitoring stack you can, for example:

- **SNMP** — Poll your router's interfaces for traffic counters, error rates, and interface status.
- **BGP monitoring** — Use tools like `bgpstream`, `exabgp`, or your router's built-in monitoring to track BGP session stability and prefix changes.
- **Ping / latency probes** — Set up regular pings or latency measurements between your on-premises network and OVHcloud resources.
- **Third-party platforms** — Tools like Datadog, Zabbix, PRTG, or Grafana can aggregate metrics from your own infrastructure.

These are generic, customer-side practices and not OVHcloud features.

## On-demand diagnostics

{/* CP-STEPS-START:on-demand-diagnostics */}
You can launch on-demand diagnostics from the OVHcloud Console to get a point-in-time status report of your OVHcloud Connect service. Each diagnostic runs in real time against the OVHcloud-side equipment and returns a result you can view or download.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:on-demand-diagnostics */}

### Available diagnostics

{/* CP-STEPS-START:available-diagnostics */}
**Layer 3 mode:**

| Diagnostic type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Default** | Fetches the BGP session state and related information. In the Control Panel this is triggered by the <code className="action">Check BGP peering</code> action on the PoP configuration. |
| **Routes** | Fetches the routing table learned by OVHcloud via BGP (routes received from your side). |
| **Advertised-Routes** | Fetches the routing table advertised by OVHcloud to your side. |

**Layer 2 mode:**

| Diagnostic type | Description |
|---|---|
| **MAC Address** | Fetches the list of MAC addresses seen on the Layer 2 segment between your network devices and the vRack. |

{/* CP-STEPS-END:available-diagnostics */}
### Launch a diagnostic

{/* CP-STEPS-START:launch-diagnostic */}
1. Open the OVHcloud Connect service you want to diagnose, on the <code className="action">Configure</code> tab.
2. In the `PoP configuration` table, click the ellipsis button <code className="action">...</code> on the port row.
3. Select the diagnostic to run — for example <code className="action">Check BGP peering</code> in Layer 3 mode, or the MAC-address diagnostic in Layer 2 mode.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:launch-diagnostic */}

### Retrieve a result

{/* CP-STEPS-START:retrieve-result */}
1. Open the <code className="action">Diagnostic</code> tab of the service. Each diagnostic is listed with its **ID Diagnostic**, **Date**, **Name**, **Scope**, and **Status**. If a diagnostic you have just launched is not listed yet, click <code className="action">Refresh</code>.
2. Once the diagnostic is complete, click the <code className="action">⋮</code> button in the **Actions** column of its row.
3. Select <code className="action">View the result</code> to open the output in the **Diagnostic result** window, or <code className="action">Download</code> to save it as a `.txt` file.
{/* CP-STEPS-END:retrieve-result */}

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| **Retention** | Only diagnostics initiated **within the last seven days** are accessible. Download and archive the ones you need to keep. |
| **Rate limit** | **10 diagnostics per type, per service, per 24 hours.** This applies independently to each diagnostic type (Default, Routes, Advertised-Routes, MAC Address). |

:::info

Diagnostics are also available programmatically through the OVHcloud API under the `/ovhCloudConnect/{serviceName}/diagnostic/...` endpoints. Refer to the [API console](https://api.ovh.com/) for the full list.

:::

## What to watch for

<img className="thumbnail" alt="What to watch for" src="/images/network/ovhcloud-connect/occ-monitor/fig-1.svg" loading="lazy" />

Watch for these conditions in the OVHcloud Console and from your own monitoring:

{/* CP-STEPS-START:what-to-watch-for */}
| Condition | How to detect it | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Link down** | Physical port state changes to "Down" (**Link status** column in the OVHcloud Console / your router) | Check cross-connect, SFP, and contact data centre or provider |
| **BGP session down** | The **BGP status** column in the OVHcloud Console changes from "Up" to "Down", or your router no longer reports the session as "Established" (you can also run the **Default** diagnostic) | Check router configuration, peer reachability, and firewall rules |
| **Prefix count change** | Unexpected increase or decrease in received/sent prefixes (run the **Routes** / **Advertised-Routes** diagnostics, check the **Incoming BGP routes** chart of the **Statistics** tab, or check your router) | Investigate route leaks or configuration changes |

{/* CP-STEPS-END:what-to-watch-for */}

## Best practices

{/* CP-STEPS-START:best-practices */}
- **Monitor from both sides** — Check the status and optical values in the OVHcloud Console, and traffic/BGP metrics on your own router.
- **Baseline your metrics** — Record normal bandwidth, latency, and prefix counts on your side so you can detect anomalies.
- **Automate alerting on your side** — In the OVHcloud Console, enable the notifications of **Notification management**: they cover interface status, BGP status changes, optical level degradation and packet loss. For other critical events, set up automated notifications in your own monitoring stack.
- **Run diagnostics when needed** — Use the on-demand diagnostics to confirm BGP state and route exchange when investigating an issue.
- **Test failover** — If your setup is redundant (several PoPs, or BGP sessions with your own router in each availability zone), periodically test failover and verify that your monitoring detects the switch.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:best-practices */}
