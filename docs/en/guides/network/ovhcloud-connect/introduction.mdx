## What is OVHcloud Connect?

OVHcloud Connect is a **dedicated, private network connection** between your infrastructure and OVHcloud. Instead of routing traffic over the public Internet, OVHcloud Connect establishes a direct link that offers better security, lower latency, and guaranteed bandwidth.

## Who is it for?

OVHcloud Connect is designed for organisations that need:

- **Reliable connectivity** for business-critical applications (ERP, databases, backups).
- **Enhanced security** by keeping traffic off the public Internet.
- **Predictable performance** with dedicated bandwidth (no shared bottlenecks).
- **Multi-cloud or hybrid-cloud** architectures connecting on-premises data centres, different cloud infrastructures, or WAN networks to OVHcloud.

## How does it work?

OVHcloud Connect links your network to OVHcloud through a **Point of Presence (PoP)** — a physical location where OVHcloud has networking equipment. You can establish this link in two ways:

| Connection type | How it works | Supported network layers | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OVHcloud Connect Direct** | You manage a physical cable (cross-connect)<br/>between your equipment and OVHcloud's<br/>equipment in a common Point of Presence. | Layer 2 (L2)<br/>and Layer 3 (L3) | Organisations already present<br/>in an OVHcloud PoP. |
| **OVHcloud Connect Provider** | A third-party network provider among our<br/>[partners](/guides/network/ovhcloud-connect/providers)<br/>handles the physical connection on your behalf. | Layer 3 (L3)<br/>only | Organisations that are not co-located with<br/>OVHcloud, or that prefer a managed<br/>connectivity service. |

### Layer 2 (L2) service

OVHcloud Connect L2 links your infrastructure to OVHcloud services at the data link layer (Layer 2). It allows you to extend your private network to OVHcloud datacenters, creating a seamless bridge between your local network and cloud resources, no routing involved. As opposed to Layer 3 service, it is transparent to VLANs (802.1q).

<img className="thumbnail" alt="OVHcloud Connect L2 implementation: the L2 tunnel is forwarded to and from the vRack" src="/images/network/ovhcloud-connect/l2/occ-l2-implementation.png" loading="lazy" />

In L2 mode, the virtual link is an L2 tunnel forwarded to and from your vRack, so Ethernet traffic is carried transparently between your equipment and OVHcloud.

**How L2 traffic is handled**

L2 forwards Ethernet frames (with their 802.1q VLAN header, if present):

- Ethernet broadcast is forwarded.
- Unknown Ethernet unicast is forwarded.
- Multicast is forwarded (treated as broadcast, limited to 20 pps).
- Point-to-point control frames such as LLDP and LACP are **not** forwarded.

Your VLANs are preserved end to end: the same VLAN IDs apply in your network and inside the OVHcloud vRack.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="OVHcloud Connect L2 traffic: the customer vRack is extended as-is and forwarded to the customer link, transparent to VLANs" src="/images/network/ovhcloud-connect/l2/occ-l2-trafic.png" loading="lazy" />

**Key points:**

- It is a strict point-to-point link (**one PoP** and **one AZ** only).
- Only **one** OVHcloud Connect L2 is supported per vRack.
- Redundancy is achieved **only** by link aggregation (LACP) on the same PoP — when two links are configured on a PoP, LACP is mandatory.
- Jumbo frames up to **9000 bytes** are supported.

**Potential use cases:**

- Simple hybrid topologies
- "Lift & Shift" migrations without IP changes
- Applications requiring L2 adjacency or VLAN transparency

For the supported and unsupported L2 designs, see [Layer 2 topologies](#layer-2-topologies) below. For the full list of capabilities and unsupported features, see [Prerequisites and limitations](/guides/network/ovhcloud-connect/limits).

### Layer 3 (L3) service

OVHcloud Connect L3 links your infrastructure to OVHcloud services at the network layer (Layer 3). It allows routing and requires routes exchange.

Once the physical link is established, the connection is associated with your **vRack** — OVHcloud's virtual private network — so your OVHcloud resources can communicate privately with your external infrastructure.

**How L3 routing is structured**

An L3 service is defined at two levels:

- **PoP level** — an L3 session with a **/30 peering subnet** between your router and OVHcloud at the Point of Presence.
- **AZ level** — how routes reach your services inside the availability zone, either through **BGP peering** or through **static routes** defined with a next-hop IP and a destination subnet.

**Choosing between BGP and static routing**

Both options are available at the AZ level, and the choice determines how routes are maintained and how traffic recovers from a failure:

| | BGP | Static routing |
|---|---|---|
| Route maintenance | Routes are learned and withdrawn automatically | You update routes yourself when your network changes |
| Multi-path | ECMP is enabled automatically with two or more PoPs (up to 4 paths) | Single path — no ECMP or traffic engineering |
| Gateway redundancy in the AZ | Handled by BGP (VRRP is disabled on that AZ configuration) | VRRP remains active and provides failover between OVHcloud devices A and B |
| Best suited to | A growing or multiple set of prefixes, or a resilient multi-path, multi-AZ design | A small, stable set of prefixes with no routing protocol |

**Key points:**

- It is supported by a Full Mesh architecture allowing multiple PoPs and availability zones (AZ) to be interconnected within a region.
- Network resilience can be created via BGP sessions (multi-peers) and ECMP, with the possibility of automatic failover between multiple PoPs.

**Potential use cases:**

- Enterprise WAN integration (Cloud as a Branch)
- Critical multi-PoP architectures
- Large-scale complex deployments.

For the full-mesh rules between PoPs and availability zones, see [Layer 3 topologies](#layer-3-topologies) below. For resilient designs, see [Multi-AZ architectures](/guides/network/ovhcloud-connect/multi-az) and [Resilient architecture](/guides/network/ovhcloud-connect/resilient-architecture).

## Supported topologies

The way you can wire PoPs and availability zones (AZ) together depends on the service layer. Understanding these rules up front helps you design a topology that matches your resiliency needs.

### Layer 2 topologies

An OCC L2 service is **strictly point-to-point**: it connects **one PoP (entry point) to one availability zone**, and nothing else. A single vRack can hold **only one OCC L2 service**.

Because the link is point-to-point, redundancy is achieved **only through link aggregation (LACP) on the same PoP**: you bundle several physical interfaces into one logical link. A backup link routed through a *second* PoP is **not supported** for L2.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="L2 point-to-point topologies: a backup link through a second PoP is not supported" src="/images/network/ovhcloud-connect/l2/occ-l2-topologies.png" loading="lazy" />

### Layer 3 topologies

An OCC L3 service builds a **full-mesh IP network between any PoP and any availability zone of the same region**. This gives you much more freedom than L2:

- You can associate **several PoPs with one AZ**, and **several AZs with one PoP**.
- You **cannot** link two PoPs together — no traffic is forwarded directly between PoPs.
- One L3 domain (subnet) **maps to a single AZ** and cannot be stretched across two AZs or two PoPs.
- You can run **as many L3 services as you want** in the same vRack.

When at least two PoPs are attached, **ECMP (Equal-Cost Multi-Path) is enabled automatically** (up to 4 paths), distributing traffic across the available links for resiliency.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="OVHcloud Connect L3 multi-datacentre topology: one PoP reaching several availability zones" src="/images/network/ovhcloud-connect/introduction/occ-l3-multidc.png" loading="lazy" />

### Mixing L2 and L3

L2 and L3 services can **coexist in the same vRack**, so you can combine a transparent L2 bridge with routed L3 connectivity when your architecture needs both.
