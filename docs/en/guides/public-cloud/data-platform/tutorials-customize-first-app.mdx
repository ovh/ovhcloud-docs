## Objective

This tutorial picks up the application you built in the Getting Started guide and adds two things to it: a chart that ranks your 7 busiest stations, and a **number format** that displays ride counts in millions of passengers rather than as long strings of digits.

You build both in the **Visual Editor** of the **Mosaic** template, as in [Expose and visualise your data](/guides/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-started-expose-and-visualize), then deploy them as a new version of your application.

## Requirements

To follow this tutorial you need the `analytics` application from [Expose and visualise your data](/guides/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-started-expose-and-visualize), with its version `v2.0.0` running.

:::info
The *Visual Editor* tab only appears on a running version. Everything below happens on the version that currently serves your users, and you copy it at the end to publish your changes.

:::

## What you will build

| Stage | Where | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Declare a number format | *Formats* in the Visual Editor | A `Rides` format that displays values in millions of passengers (`Mpax`) |
| 2. Add a heading and a ranked chart | The *Home* page of the application | A *Top 7 stations* bar chart that uses the `Rides` format |
| 3. Deploy a new version | **Applications & APIs** | Version `v3.0.0`, which replaces `v2.0.0` without interruption |

## Instructions

### Step 1: Opening the Visual Editor

Open **Applications & APIs**, then your `analytics` application, then its version `v2.0.0`, and go to the *Visual Editor* tab.

The editor opens on *Home*, the page that the Mosaic template creates in every application. It holds two containers: *Filters* at the top, and an empty container below it. This tutorial fills the empty one. The *Rides analytics* page you built in the Getting Started guide stays as it is.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The Home page in the Visual Editor, with two empty containers, Filters at the top" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/01-home-dashboard.png" loading="lazy" />

### Step 2: Declaring a number format

Your ride counts run into the millions, and a chart that prints `25833640` is hard to read. A number format fixes that once for the whole application.

Click **Formats** in the toolbar. The *Number and date formats* dialog lists the formats the application already has, such as percentages and shortened numbers.

Under *Measures*, enter `Rides` in the name field and click **Add**. Then fill in the new entry:

| Field | Value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | `Mpax` | Appended after the number: millions of passengers |
| Multiplier | `0.000001` | Turns a number of rides into millions |
| Decimals | `2` | Rounds to 2 decimals |

Leave *Shorten above* and *Suffixes* empty. Next to the name of the entry, a preview shows a sample value in this format, `1.23Mpax`: check it before you save.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The Rides entry in the Number and date formats dialog: Mpax, 0.000001, 2 decimals" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/02-formatter-measure.png" loading="lazy" />

Click **Save**, then **Close**.

:::info
The formats are saved in the sources of the version, in `config/formatter.json`, and are shared by every chart of the application. They travel with the version when you copy it in Step 5.

:::

### Step 3: Adding a heading

Point at the empty container below *Filters* and click its **+ Add** button, then select **Basic** > **HTML**.

The *HTML options* panel opens on the right. In *HTML*, enter:

```html
<h1 class='text-xl'>Top 7 Stations</h1>
```

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The Top 7 Stations heading in the container, with the HTML options panel open" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/03-title-block.png" loading="lazy" />

:::warning
The content of an HTML block is injected as it is. Anyone who can edit the dashboard can run a script in the session of every reader, so only give edit access to people you trust.

:::

The **+ Add** button of the container only shows while your pointer is over the container.

### Step 4: Adding the ranked chart

Click **+ Add** in the same container again and select **Charts** > **Bar horizontal**.

In the *General* tab, name the chart `Top 7 stations`.

Open the *Data* tab. Leave **Data source** on *Query builder*, then describe the ranking:

- Under *Measures*, click **+ Add** and select `rides` with the `sum` aggregation.
- Under *Group by*, click **+ Add** and select `station_name`.
- Under *Order by*, click **+ a criterion** and select `rides`, `descending`.
- In *Row limit*, enter `7`, to keep only the 7 busiest stations.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The chart's Data tab: sum of rides by station_name, descending, limited to 7 rows" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/04-chart-data.png" loading="lazy" />

Open the *Format* tab. It lists the fields of the chart. On the `rides` row, select the `Rides` format you declared in Step 2: the preview reads `1.23Mpax`. The axis and the tooltips of the chart now use it.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The chart's Format tab, with the Rides format selected for the rides field" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/05-chart-format.png" loading="lazy" />

Click **Apply**. The chart queries your data through your API and draws the 7 busiest stations, with an axis in `Mpax`.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The Top 7 stations bar chart under the heading, with its axis in Mpax" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/06-chart-added.png" loading="lazy" />

Click **Save** in the toolbar. The counter next to it goes back to *no changes*.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The saved dashboard with its heading and chart, the toolbar counter showing no changes" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/07-dashboard-saved.png" loading="lazy" />

### Step 5: Deploying a new version

Saving writes your changes into the sources of `v2.0.0`, but your users are still served the build made before them. To publish them, copy the running version:

1. Go back to the overview of your application.
2. On the `v2.0.0` row, open the **…** menu and select **Copy**.
3. Name the copy `v3.0.0`, tick **Auto Build** and **Auto Deploy**, and click **Create**.

    <img className="thumbnail" alt="The Versions list: v3.0.0 just created, v2.0.0 deployed and marked Rebuild needed, v1.0.0 stopped" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/08-version-copied.png" loading="lazy" />

`v2.0.0` keeps serving your users while `v3.0.0` builds and deploys. Data Platform switches to `v3.0.0` once it is ready, then stops `v2.0.0`.

The build takes a couple of minutes. To follow it, open the *Builds* tab of the version and click the logs icon. The *Build Logs* dialog shows the state of the build, its time in the queue and each step as it runs.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="The Build Logs dialog of version v3.0.0, in progress, streaming the first build steps" src="/images/public-cloud/data-platform/getting-further/customize-first-app/picts/09-build-logs.png" loading="lazy" />

### Step 6: Opening your application

Once `v3.0.0` is running, open your application and sign in with **Data Platform**. *Home* now shows the *Top 7 Stations* heading and the ranked chart, with its figures in millions of passengers.

## What you have built

Your application has a number format shared by all its charts, and a ranked chart on its *Home* page, deployed as version `v3.0.0` without interrupting your users. You can reuse the same format in every chart that displays rides.
