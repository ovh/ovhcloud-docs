## Objective

This guide explains how pricing works for OVHcloud Public Cloud Databases. It covers the three main cost components (compute, storage and backups), how per-second billing is applied, and how to interpret pricing across the website, order page and billing usage in the OVHcloud Control Panel.

## Database pricing components

### Compute (virtual machine)

Compute represents the virtual machine running your database service. It includes CPU and RAM resources, which directly impact performance, scalability and the overall hourly cost of your service.

### Storage (Block Storage)

Storage corresponds to the Block Storage volume where your database data is stored. This component is billed based on the allocated capacity (in GB) and is independent from compute resources.

Databases such as Valkey are in-memory systems, meaning they store data primarily in RAM and therefore do not directly benefit from Block Storage for their core data operations.

### Backups (Object Storage)

Backups are stored in Object Storage and ensure data durability and recovery. Pricing depends not only on the database engine and any associated retention policy, but also on the nature of the data files and how the database engine structures and organizes the data—while backup tools may optimize the final backup size through compression, these factors still directly influence the characteristics and overall size of the resulting backups.

## Billing and pricing principles

### Per-second billing

Public Cloud Databases are billed on a per-second basis: you only pay for the exact duration your resources are used. This provides flexibility and ensures accurate cost tracking, especially for short-lived or scaled workloads.

### Hourly price (source of truth)

The hourly price is the reference value used to calculate costs. It reflects the price of your resources per hour and serves as the basis for all billing calculations.

### Monthly price

The monthly price is an estimation derived from the hourly price:

- Monthly price = Hourly price × 730 hours

This gives a standard approximation of monthly costs for comparison and planning purposes, even though actual billing remains based on per-second usage.

### Invoice structure

Invoices for database services are designed to clearly reflect how resources are billed.

Up until May 2026, an invoice for a given service typically includes two billing lines:

- The database service itself (covering compute, base storage and backups)
- Optional additional storage, if provisioned

As the billing model evolves toward greater transparency, invoices are being updated to include three distinct lines:

- The compute component of the service
- The total storage usage, combining base storage and any additional storage
- The backups stored for the service

This evolution provides better visibility into how costs are distributed between compute, storage and backup resources.

## Pricing visibility across interfaces

### Website pricing page

The [pricing page on the OVHcloud website](https://www.ovhcloud.com/en-gb/public-cloud/prices/) provides a high-level overview of database service costs. It displays hourly and estimated monthly prices, allowing users to compare different plans and configurations.

### OVHcloud Control Panel – Order page

When ordering a database service in the OVHcloud Control Panel, pricing is displayed in real time based on selected resources. The estimate updates dynamically as you adjust compute, storage or backup options.

### OVHcloud Control Panel – Billing & usage

In the billing section of the OVHcloud Control Panel, you can monitor your actual usage and costs. Charges are detailed per component and reflect the per-second billing model, giving full transparency into how your bill is calculated.

## Billing example

The following example illustrates how billing quantities are calculated and aggregated for multiple database services over a billing period.

### Scenario

During the month of April (30 days), the following services are running:

| Service       | Plan       | Flavor | Base storage | Additional storage | Creation date   |
| ------------- | ---------- | ------ | ------------ | ------------------ | --------------- |
| PostgreSQL #1 | Production | B3-8   | 160 GB       | 100 GB             | 1 April, 00:00  |
| PostgreSQL #2 | Production | B3-32  | 640 GB       | None               | 15 April, 00:00 |
| MongoDB #1    | Production | B3-16  | 80 GB        | 120 GB             | 1 April, 00:00  |

### Previous billing model

Under the previous billing model, compute and additional storage were billed using separate service-specific references.

#### PostgreSQL #1

| Reference                                      |         Quantity | Computation                           |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------: | ------------------------------------- |
| `postgresql-production-b3-8`                   |      1,440 hours | 30 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes          |
| `postgresql-production-additionnal-storage-gb` | 144,000 GB/hours | 100 GB × 30 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes |

#### PostgreSQL #2

| Reference                     |  Quantity | Computation                  |
| ----------------------------- | --------: | ---------------------------- |
| `postgresql-production-b3-32` | 720 hours | 15 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes |

#### MongoDB #1

| Reference                                   |         Quantity | Computation                           |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------: | ------------------------------------- |
| `mongodb-production-b3-16`                  |      2,160 hours | 30 days × 24 hours × 3 nodes          |
| `mongodb-production-additionnal-storage-gb` | 259,200 GB/hours | 120 GB × 30 days × 24 hours × 3 nodes |

#### Aggregated bill

The bill aggregates quantities by reference:

| Reference                                      |   Total quantity |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------: |
| `postgresql-production-b3-8`                   |      1,440 hours |
| `postgresql-production-additionnal-storage-gb` | 144,000 GB/hours |
| `postgresql-production-b3-32`                  |        720 hours |
| `mongodb-production-b3-16`                     |      2,160 hours |
| `mongodb-production-additionnal-storage-gb`    | 259,200 GB/hours |

### New billing model

Under the new billing model, compute, storage and backup are billed separately.

Storage references include both the default storage included with the flavor and any additional storage attached to the service.

Backup references correspond to the storage capacity protected by automated backups.

#### PostgreSQL #1

| Reference                            |         Quantity | Computation                           |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------: | ------------------------------------- |
| `postgresql-production-b3-8-compute` |      1,440 hours | 30 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes          |
| `postgresql-production-storage-gb`   | 374,400 GB/hours | 260 GB × 30 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes |
| `postgresql-production-backup-gb`    | 187,200 GB/hours | 260 GB × 30 days × 24 hours           |

#### PostgreSQL #2

| Reference                             |         Quantity | Computation                           |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------: | ------------------------------------- |
| `postgresql-production-b3-32-compute` |        720 hours | 15 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes          |
| `postgresql-production-storage-gb`    | 460,800 GB/hours | 640 GB × 15 days × 24 hours × 2 nodes |
| `postgresql-production-backup-gb`     | 230,400 GB/hours | 640 GB × 15 days × 24 hours           |

#### MongoDB #1

| Reference                          |         Quantity | Computation                           |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------: | ------------------------------------- |
| `mongodb-production-b3-16-compute` |      2,160 hours | 30 days × 24 hours × 3 nodes          |
| `mongodb-production-storage-gb`    | 432,000 GB/hours | 200 GB × 30 days × 24 hours × 3 nodes |
| `mongodb-production-backup-gb`     | 144,000 GB/hours | 200 GB × 30 days × 24 hours           |

#### Aggregated bill

References shared by multiple services are aggregated on the bill.

For example, both PostgreSQL services contribute to the `postgresql-production-storage-gb` and `postgresql-production-backup-gb` references.

| Reference                             |   Total quantity |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------: |
| `postgresql-production-b3-8-compute`  |      1,440 hours |
| `postgresql-production-b3-32-compute` |        720 hours |
| `postgresql-production-storage-gb`    | 835,200 GB/hours |
| `postgresql-production-backup-gb`     | 417,600 GB/hours |
| `mongodb-production-b3-16-compute`    |      2,160 hours |
| `mongodb-production-storage-gb`       | 432,000 GB/hours |
| `mongodb-production-backup-gb`        | 144,000 GB/hours |

In this example:

- `postgresql-production-storage-gb` is the sum of:

  - 374,400 GB/hours from PostgreSQL #1
  - 460,800 GB/hours from PostgreSQL #2

- Total: **835,200 GB/hours**

- `postgresql-production-backup-gb` is the sum of:

  - 187,200 GB/hours from PostgreSQL #1
  - 230,400 GB/hours from PostgreSQL #2

- Total: **417,600 GB/hours**

This aggregation mechanism lets a single billing reference represent the combined usage of multiple services of the same engine and plan.
