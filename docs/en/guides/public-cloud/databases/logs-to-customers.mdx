## Objective

Public Cloud managed databases allow you to send logs of your service to your own Logs Data Platform (LDP) data stream.

**This guide explains how to forward service logs to your own LDP stream with the OVHcloud API.**

## Requirements

- A [Public Cloud database service](/links/public-cloud/databases) up and running
- Access to the <ApiLink>OVHcloud API</ApiLink>
- A Logs Data Platform account within this OVHcloud account with at least one destination stream configured
    - If you are not familiar with all the LDP *Stream* configuration possibilities, simply create a new one with the default options (indexing & websocket enabled, long-term storage disabled) for the purpose of this guide.
- A running database service

## Instructions

### Create your subscription

<Tabs>
  <Tab label="Via the OVHcloud Console">
    On your database service page, open the <code className="action">Observability</code> tab, then the **Logs** view. Select the **Log kind**, then click <code className="action">Manage streaming</code>.

    In the **Manage log streaming** panel, under **Subscribe to a stream**, select your **LDP account** and the **Stream**, then click <code className="action">Subscribe</code>.

    The logs will then start to be forwarded to your LDP stream.
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Via the OVHcloud API">
    1\. Retrieve the required information
    
    **Retrieve your LDP destination `streamId`:**
    
    In the OVHcloud Console, open <code className="action">Observability</code> > <code className="action">Logs Management</code> in the left-hand menu, select your Logs Data Platform account, then open the <code className="action">Data streams</code> tab.
    
    Click the name of your target stream. The stream ID is the identifier that follows `/streams/` in the address of the page that opens (`.../logs-management/<ldp-account>/streams/<streamId>/dashboard`): copy it.
    
    **Retrieve your LDP destination `serviceName`:**
    
    - This refers to your Public Cloud project ID. To copy it, click the <code className="action">Project</code> selector at the top of the left-hand menu of your Public Cloud project, then click <code className="action">Copy project id</code> on the row of your project.
    
**Retrieve your `clusterId`:**
    
    In the left-hand menu of your Public Cloud project, click your engine under **Databases**, then click the name of the service you want to manage.
    
    In the **General information** card of the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab, you can find the **Service ID** field, which corresponds to the cluster ID.
    
    2\. Start by retrieving the types of logs available for your database cluster with the following API call:
    
    <Api version="v1" section="/cloud" method="GET" route={"/cloud/project/\\{serviceName\\}/database/mysql/\\{clusterId\\}/log/kind"} />

Replace `mysql` in the route with your service's engine (for example `postgresql` or `mongodb`).

This will return the list of valid kind values you can use when subscribing to logs.
    
    3\. Once you know the valid kind, use it to subscribe to a log stream with this API call:
    
    <Api version="v1" section="/cloud" method="POST" route={"/cloud/project/\\{serviceName\\}/database/mysql/\\{clusterId\\}/log/subscription"} />

```console
    body : {
        kind: <log_type_from_previous_call>
        streamId: <your_stream_id>
    }
```
    
    The logs will then start to be forwarded to your LDP stream.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>


### Find logs in Graylog

On the LDP page, click the <code className="action">sample-data-stream</code> button (the last data stream modified when you previously subscribed to it), or click the <code className="action">Graylog</code> button.

You need to log in using your Graylog credentials. You can retrieve them from the LDP details page, in the `Configuration` section. The login corresponds to the value shown on the `Logs Data Platform service` line, and the password is displayed under `Password`.

Once connected, you can view your service logs in your Graylog stream.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="LDP database graylogs result" src="/images/public-cloud/databases/databases-16-logs-to-customer/ldp_database_graylogs_result.png" loading="lazy" />

You can also use the following Graylog queries for more granular filtering:

#### MongoDB

Query: `cluster: "<HostID>"`

You can find this `HostID` in the OVHcloud Console:

- Open the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab of your MongoDB service
- In the **Connection details** card, the **Host** field has the format `<HostID>.database.cloud.ovh.net`

#### Other Engines

Query: `clusterID: "<Engine>-<HostID>"`

You can find this `HostID` in the OVHcloud Console:

- Find the **Service ID** (in the **General information** card of the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab), formatted as a UUID (AAAAAAAA-BBBB-CCCC-DDDDDDDDDDDD)
- `HostID` is the first part of the UUID (AAAAAAAA)

### Delete subscription

You have 2 methods to delete a subscription:

<Tabs>
  <Tab label="Via the OVHcloud Console">
    In the **Manage log streaming** panel of the <code className="action">Observability</code> tab (**Logs** view > <code className="action">Manage streaming</code>), click <code className="action">Unsubscribe</code> on the subscription in **Current subscriptions**, then confirm.
  </Tab>
  <Tab label="Via the OVHcloud API">
    - You can delete subscriptions using the `subscriptionId` concerned in this API call:
    
    <Api version="v1" section="/cloud" method="DELETE" route={"/cloud/project/\\{serviceName\\}/database/mysql/\\{clusterId\\}/log/subscription/\\{subscriptionId\\}"} />
  </Tab>
</Tabs>


- If you delete your database service, all subscriptions of this service are deleted automatically.
