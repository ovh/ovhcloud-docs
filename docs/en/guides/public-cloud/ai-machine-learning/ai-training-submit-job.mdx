## Objective

This guide covers the initialisation of **AI Training** and the submission of [**jobs**](/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-training-jobs) through the OVHcloud Console.

## Requirements

-   a **Public cloud** project
-   optionally container objects to attach data to the **job** at step 8, see our [create data container](/guides/storage-and-backup/object-storage/pcs-create-container) guide.

[[cpnav:publiccloud-ai-training|en]]

## Instructions

### Step 1 - Going to the AI Training menu

In the left-hand menu of your Public Cloud project, click <code className="action">AI Training</code> under **AI**.

### Step 2 - Starting a job submission

If the project has not been authorised for AI services yet, click <code className="action">Authorise the project</code> in the **Authorise this project for AI services** banner. Upon authorising the project you grant OVHcloud access to your Object Storage containers. This access is only used to synchronise your data within **AI Training** with your containers.

From the **jobs** list you can start the job submission by clicking the <code className="action">Create a job</code> button. The **Create a job** page opens: a summary of your configuration and its estimated price per hour is displayed on the right-hand side.

### Step 3 - Naming your job and selecting a region

Give a name to your job in the **Name** section. This will make it easier to manage when you have multiple jobs running.

Each **job** is executed in an OVHcloud region. Each region has its own **AI Training** cluster with potentially varying capabilities. For more information, see the [capabilities](/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-training-capabilities).

Select the desired region in the **Region** section.

### Step 4 - Specifying the amount of resources

In the **Flavor** section, select the CPU or GPU flavor you need for your training workload, then adjust the **Quantity**.

The max amount of GPUs or CPUs you can select for your **job** is region-dependent. If you choose a GPU, a fixed ratio of CPU is applied based on the number of GPUs. Similarly, there is a fixed ratio of Memory based on the number of CPUs. For more information see the [capabilities](/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-training-capabilities).

Once the amount of resources is set you can see a preview of the billing rate in the summary.

### Step 5 - Providing a Docker image

A job is basically a Docker container that is run within the OVHcloud infrastructure. You need to provide a Docker image to be executed in the **Docker image** section. There are several options you can choose from:

#### Preset Images

With **Ready-to-use image catalogue**, OVHcloud provides a set of images from which you can choose to ease the submission of your first **jobs**. Provided images are essentially a JupyterLab environment bundled with some Deep Learning technology such as Tensorflow or MXNet. These images are planned to be removed, as the product is more focused on using custom images.

#### Custom Images

Preset images cannot cover all your needs so you can specify your own image if necessary with **Custom image**. You can use any image that is accessible from **AI Training**.

This includes public images (e.g. Dockerhub), images within the shared registry or images in your added private registry. For more information, see how to [add a private registry](/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-manage-registries).

### Step 6 - Privacy Settings

Next, select your privacy settings in the **Privacy** section: **Private** (access requires a token) or **Public** (access without a token).

:::warning
*Public access* will expose your data and code to anyone getting the AI Training job link. Be careful and don't use it with sensitive data. On the other hand, *Private access* will ask a user and password combination or an AI token to access the job content, ensuring a secure environment.

:::

### Step 7 - Job lifecycle

:::warning
Actually our main motivation is to keep the plateform up-to-date, in matter of security patches and new features alignment, so we need to update and restart hosts from time to time, which requires to get them free from any customer workload.
This also allows us to address host-located incidents, e.g. when a single GPU fails due to faulty hardware (only 1 customer impacted, but we still need to stop the host for bringing it to PCI maintenance, so every remaining workload must be evicted or rescheduled elsewhere).

:::

By default, your job will automatically shut down after 7 consecutive days of being in `RUNNING` status (**Expiration** section). Rest assured that all your settings and data will be preserved. You have the option to turn on **Automatically restart after timeout**, which will automatically restart your job every 7 days, ensuring minimal disruption to your workflow. Alternatively, you can also contact [our support](/links/support-contact) to extend this automatic restart period.

### Step 8 - Advanced configuration

*This step is optional.*

In the **Advanced configuration** section, turn on the switch of each option you need.

First, the Docker image you provided in Step 5 includes an entrypoint for your container. You can override this entrypoint by specifying your own command (**Commands**).

Then, by default, your AI Training job comes with **ephemeral storage** (local storage). But in this step, you can also link Object Storage containers (**Volumes**) and Git repositories (**Git Public**) to your job, either as input for your training workload or as output for your results (e.g. model weights).

If you want to learn more about configuring containers and Git repositories in the job, you can refer to this [model training example](/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-training-train-first-ml-model). For now, we will launch a classic job without any external volumes added to it.

You can also add **Labels** to your job.

Finally, **SSH key** allows you to access your job remotely.

### Step 9 - Submitting your job

Before submission, check the overview of the **job** you configured in the summary. Click <code className="action">CLI equivalent</code> to get the equivalent command to use with the **`ovhai` CLI**.

Click <code className="action">Create the job</code> to confirm and launch the creation of your **job** to the cluster.

When your job is created, it will appear in the list of your AI Training jobs.

### Step 10 - Consulting your job

From this list you can access your job details by clicking on its name. The <code className="action">Overview</code> tab includes several components, like the job resources, status, life cycle and access URL (**Connection details** card). This URL is of the form `https://<JOB-ID>.job.<REGION>.ai.cloud.ovh.net/`. Moreover, you can access any available port by adding it to the job URL this way: `https://<JOB-ID>-<PORT>.job.<REGION>.ai.cloud.ovh.net/`.

You can also check your job logs in the <code className="action">Observability</code> tab.

### Step 11 - Stopping your job

If you are done using your job, if your model converged prematurely or if you just wish to interrupt your job you can do so from the **jobs** list and the job page.

From the list of **jobs**, click the <code className="action">...</code> button at the far right of the entry and interrupt the job by clicking <code className="action">Stop</code>. Alternatively, from the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab of the **job**, click <code className="action">Stop</code> in the **General information** card. Confirm with <code className="action">Stop</code>: the run is interrupted and cannot be resumed.

After that, if you no longer need your job, you can delete it. To do so, click the <code className="action">...</code> button on the row of the job, then select <code className="action">Delete</code> (or click <code className="action">Delete job</code> in the **General information** card) and confirm.

### Step 12 - Cloning your job

You can also click <code className="action">Clone job</code> (from the <code className="action">...</code> button of the job list or the **General information** card) to clone the job with the same configuration. This will launch a new job identical to the original (e.g., `example-job`), allowing you to rerun your workflow without re‑configuring the job.

## Go further
The **AI Training** service is mainly supposed to be used through the **`ovhai` CLI**. The OVHcloud Console only offers a subset of the features and is meant to help you get started before using the CLI. Discover how to [install the OVHcloud AI CLI](/guides/public-cloud/ai-machine-learning/ai-cli-install-client).
