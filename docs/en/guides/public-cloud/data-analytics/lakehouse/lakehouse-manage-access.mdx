## Objective

Every access to the data of a Lakehouse goes through a user, its roles and a credential.

**This guide explains how to organise your data in catalogs and schemas, give users access to it and manage their credentials.**

## Requirements

- A Lakehouse in your Public Cloud project. See [Lakehouse - Getting started](/guides/public-cloud/data-analytics/lakehouse/lakehouse-getting-started).
- Access to the <ManagerLink to="/">OVHcloud Control Panel</ManagerLink>.

## Instructions

In the OVHcloud Control Panel, open your Public Cloud project, go to **Analytics** > **Lakehouse** and click the name of your Lakehouse.

### Catalogs and schemas

Open the **Catalogs** tab to list the catalogs of the Lakehouse.

![Catalogs tab with two catalogs](/images/guides/public-cloud/data-analytics/lakehouse/manage-access/catalogs.png)

- To add a catalog, click **Add a catalog**, enter its name and an optional description, and confirm. The name cannot be changed afterwards: engines connect to the catalog by this name.
- To add a schema, click the name of a catalog, then **Add a schema**.

A catalog or a schema is available after a few seconds.

### Users and service accounts

Open the **Users** tab and click **Add a user**. Choose the type:

- **User**, for a person;
- **Service account**, for an application or a pipeline.

A new user has no access to data until you assign it a role.

### Groups

Groups let you assign roles once for several users. Open the **Groups** tab, click **Add a group**, then click the name of the group and **Add a member**. A member gets every role assigned to the group.

### Roles

Open the **Roles** tab to see the roles of the Lakehouse.

![Roles tab with the six default roles](/images/guides/public-cloud/data-analytics/lakehouse/manage-access/roles.png)

The default roles are the following:

| Role | Gives |
|---|---|
| **Lakehouse Admin** | Every privilege on the data of all catalogs, including those created later. |
| **Data Owner** | Read and modify the data of all catalogs, and create, modify and delete their tables and views. |
| **Data Editor** | Read and modify the data of all catalogs, and create and modify their tables and views, without deleting them. |
| **Data Viewer** | Read the data and metadata of all catalogs. |
| **Metadata Viewer** | See the names of the schemas, tables and views of all catalogs, without reading the data. |
| **User Admin** | No privilege on the data. |

#### Assigning a role

1. In the **Users** or **Groups** tab, open the actions menu (`...`) of the user or the group and click **Manage access**.
2. Under **Which role do you want to give?**, choose the role.
3. To limit a default role to one catalog, choose this catalog as the scope. Otherwise, keep the role as it is defined.
4. Click **Assign the role**.

The assignment is applied within a few seconds. A role limited to a catalog gives access to this catalog only: credentials issued afterwards cover only this catalog, and reading a table of another catalog is refused.

#### Creating a custom role

When the default roles do not fit, click **Add a custom role** in the **Roles** tab.

![Form to create a custom role with its scope and actions](/images/guides/public-cloud/data-analytics/lakehouse/manage-access/role-custom.png)

A custom role is a list of permissions. For each permission, choose:

- the **Scope**: **Whole Lakehouse**, one **Catalog** or one **Schema**;
- the actions: **Read** (`BROWSE`, `SELECT`), **Write** (`INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `DELETE`) and **Structure** (`CREATE_TABLE`, `ALTER`, `DROP`), or individual privileges in the detail below.

Click **Add a permission** to combine several scopes, then **Create role**. Saving the role replaces all its permissions.

### Credentials

A credential connects a tool to the Lakehouse with the rights of the roles of one user.

To create one, open the actions menu (`...`) of the user, click **Manage credentials**, then **Create a credential**. Choose:

- the **Validity**: 7, 30, 60 or 90 days;
- **Generate an S3 access key**, only for a tool that reads or writes the files of the bucket directly.

The client secret and the S3 secret key are shown only once, in the dialog that follows the creation. A user can hold up to three active credentials.

To revoke a credential, click **Revoke** in its row, or **Revoke all** to revoke every credential of the user. A revoked credential is refused within a second by the SQL endpoint and the Iceberg REST catalog.

:::tip
Give each tool or pipeline its own service account and credential. You can then revoke one access without disturbing the others.
:::
