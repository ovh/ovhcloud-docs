## Objective

**This guide describes the vSAN storage of your Private VCF as-a-Service cluster: what OVHcloud configures at delivery, what you can set yourself, and what each setting means for your VMs.**

Your cluster is delivered with a **vSAN ESA** datastore already configured: your hosts' disks are claimed, the datastore is created and, in 3-AZ, the cluster is stretched over two data AZs with a witness. There is nothing to enable. What remains is to set your **storage policies** so that your VMs get the protection you expect.

:::warning
On a 3-AZ cluster, a VM is protected against the loss of an AZ only if its storage policy mirrors its data **across both sites**. Check this for every VM that must survive the loss of an AZ (see the "[Storage policies](#storage-policies)" section).

:::

## Requirements

- A delivered [Private VCF as-a-Service](/links/hosted-private-cloud/private-vcf-aas-3-az) Workload Domain.
- Access to your vCenter with your administrator account.

## Instructions

### What is delivered

Each cluster of your Workload Domain has its own vSAN datastore, built from the disks of its hosts.

| Item | Delivered configuration |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | vSAN ESA (Express Storage Architecture). The hosts' disks are claimed automatically at delivery. |
| **Datastore** | One per cluster, named `<cluster>-ds-vsan01`. |
| **Default policy** | The one vSAN ESA manages automatically for the datastore (auto-policy management). It applies to every VM you do not assign another policy to. |
| **Minimum size** | 3 hosts, the vSAN minimum (6 in 3-AZ: 3 in each data AZ). Removing a host from a 3-host cluster is refused: to go below that, delete the cluster. |
| **Replacing a failed host** | OVHcloud replaces it with a spare host. The replacement is complete only once the vSAN resynchronisation has finished and the objects comply with their policy. |

In 3-AZ, the cluster is always a **vSAN stretched cluster**: at least 3 hosts in each of the two data AZs, each holding a complete copy of the data, and a witness in the third AZ. The architecture and the failure behaviour are described in the "[3-AZ Stretched Cluster concepts](/guides/hosted-private-cloud/powered-by-vmware/private-vcf/3az-stretched-cluster-concepts)" guide. Keep in mind that:

- OVHcloud assigns the AZs: you do not choose which one is preferred.
- the witness is a VM managed by OVHcloud in the third AZ. It stores no VM data and runs no VM.
- the witness traffic is separate from the hosts' vSAN traffic.

### What you can do, and what stays with OVHcloud

Your administrator role in vCenter lets you manage your VMs' storage. The configuration of the vSAN cluster itself stays with OVHcloud.

| You can | Stays with OVHcloud |
|---|---|
| Create, edit and apply storage policies to your VMs and their disks. | The cluster configuration: vSAN services, claimed disks, vSAN network, stretching and witness. |
| Browse the datastore, upload and manage files on it (ISOs, templates, etc.). | Replacing a failed host. |
| Monitor vSAN capacity, performance and health in vCenter. | VCF updates and patches, vSAN included. |
| Put a host into maintenance mode. | The vSphere and vSAN licences of the hosts. |
| In 3-AZ, add your VMs to the per-AZ VM groups (see the "[Placing your VMs per AZ](#placing-your-vms-per-az)" section). | |
| Add and remove hosts from the OVHcloud Control Panel. | |

:::warning
Put a host into maintenance mode before removing it: otherwise the removal is refused.

When you put a host into maintenance mode, vCenter asks what to do with its vSAN data. An option that migrates nothing leaves some objects with a single copy while the host is away: keep it for short interventions.

:::

### Storage policies {#storage-policies}

A storage policy tells vSAN how many copies of a VM disk to keep, and where. The policy, not the datastore, decides how each VM is protected.

| vSAN setting | What it decides | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Failures to tolerate** (FTT) | How many hosts can fail without data loss, and the method: mirroring (RAID-1) or erasure coding (RAID-5/6). | More tolerance = more capacity consumed. |
| **Site disaster tolerance** (3-AZ) | Whether data is copied to both data AZs ("dual site mirroring") or kept in one. | Dual site mirroring doubles consumption, but protects the data against the loss of an AZ. |
| **Provisioning** | Thin by default: space is consumed as data is written. | Watch the actual fill rate. |

In 3-AZ, the policy decides what happens to the VM if the AZ hosting it fails:

| VM policy | Loss of the AZ hosting it |
|---|---|
| Data mirrored across **both sites** | vSphere HA restarts the VM in the other AZ. **No data lost.** |
| Data kept in **a single site** | The VM and its data are unavailable for as long as the AZ is down. |

:::info
**To do after delivery**

- In vCenter, open <code className="action">Policies and Profiles</code> > <code className="action">VM Storage Policies</code> and look at the default policy of the `<cluster>-ds-vsan01` datastore.
- Check that it mirrors across both sites if your VMs must survive the loss of an AZ. If not, create a suitable policy and apply it to those VMs.
- For a VM that does not need this protection (test environment, rebuildable VM), a single-site policy saves half the capacity.

:::

#### Changing the policy of an existing VM

You can change a VM's policy while it is running. vSAN then rebuilds its objects to match the new policy: this temporarily consumes capacity and bandwidth. Avoid changing the policy of many VMs at the same time.

### 3-AZ: placing your VMs per AZ {#placing-your-vms-per-az}

For each data AZ, OVHcloud creates a host group, an empty VM group and a "should run on" DRS rule in vCenter. All you have to do is put your VMs in the groups.

| Object in vCenter | Role |
|---|---|
| `<cluster>-<zone>` | Group of the AZ's hosts, kept up to date by OVHcloud. |
| `<cluster>-<zone>-vms` | VM group of the AZ, **empty at delivery**: you add your VMs to it. |
| `<cluster>-<zone>-should-run` | DRS rule: the VMs of the group **should** run on the AZ's hosts. |

Put your VMs in these groups to:

- keep VMs that exchange a lot of traffic in the same AZ;
- split the members of an application cluster across the two AZs, so that an AZ failure does not stop all of them.

A VM outside any group can run in either AZ, as DRS decides.

The rule is a "should" rule, not a "must" rule: if no host of its AZ is running, the VM restarts in the other AZ. It only decides where the VM runs: its data follows its [storage policy](#storage-policies).

:::warning
Do not modify the host groups or the rules: OVHcloud keeps them up to date when hosts are added or replaced. Put your VMs in the VM groups and leave the rest as it is.

:::

### Capacity and growth

vSAN capacity grows with the hosts.

| Point | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Actual consumption** | A VM consumes its size multiplied by the overhead of its policy: ×2 in RAID-1 with FTT=1, ×3 with FTT=2; erasure coding costs less, about ×1.25 to ×1.5 in RAID-5 and ×1.5 in RAID-6. A dual site mirroring policy doubles this overhead: one copy per AZ. |
| **Loss of an AZ** | The remaining AZ must have enough free CPU and memory to run every VM that must keep running. |
| **Free space** | Keep free space on the datastore: vSAN needs it to rebuild objects after a failure or during maintenance. |
| **Adding capacity** | Add hosts from the OVHcloud Control Panel: in 3-AZ, in pairs, one in each data AZ, so that both AZs keep the same capacity. Their disks join the datastore. |
