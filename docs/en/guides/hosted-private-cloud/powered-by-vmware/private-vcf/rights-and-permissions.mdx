## Objective

**This guide describes your users' rights on your Private VCF as-a-Service Workload Domain: the two access levels, what they allow in vCenter and NSX, and what stays managed by OVHcloud.**

:::info
Private VCF as-a-Service is in Beta. The rights model described here may still change, and this guide will be updated accordingly.
:::

## Requirements

- A delivered [Private VCF as-a-Service](/links/hosted-private-cloud/private-vcf-aas-3-az) Workload Domain.

## Instructions

### Two access levels

Your Workload Domain has its own vCenter and its own NSX Manager, which serve you alone. Your users hold one of two access levels, the same in vCenter and in NSX.

| Level | Role in vCenter | Role in NSX | What it allows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Administrator** | `OVHcloud Customer Admin` | `ovhcloud_customer_admin` | Managing your VMs, their storage and your networks, within the limits described below. |
| **Reader** | `OVHcloud Customer Read-only` | `ovhcloud_customer_readonly` | Seeing everything an administrator sees, without being able to change anything. |

The reader role is built from the administrator role: it keeps all of its viewing rights. A reader can therefore browse the datastores, check the state of VMs or read the firewall rules, unlike vCenter's built-in `Read-only` role, which is much narrower.

You cannot grant rights yourself in vCenter or NSX: the roles carry no right to manage permissions. Access is granted through OVHcloud IAM (see [Granting access to a user](#granting-access)).

### What an administrator can do

**In vCenter:**

| Area | What you can do |
|---|---|
| **Virtual machines** | Create, configure, migrate, clone and delete your VMs, manage their snapshots, interact with the guest operating system. |
| **Storage** | Manage your datastores and storage policies, browse the datastores, upload and manage files on them. |
| **Hosts** | Put a host into maintenance mode, for example before removing it. |
| **DRS** | Manage the cluster's DRS configuration: automation level, affinity and anti-affinity rules, VM groups. In 3-AZ, do not modify the host groups or the rules created by OVHcloud (see [Managing your cluster's vSAN storage](/guides/hosted-private-cloud/powered-by-vmware/private-vcf/vsan-storage#placing-your-vms-per-az)). |
| **Encryption** | Encrypt your VMs with the keys and the encryption policy in place. |
| **Organisation** | Manage tags, tag categories and content libraries. |

**In NSX:**

| Area | What you can do |
|---|---|
| **T0 and T1 gateways** | Create and configure your gateways, their uplinks and their routing: static, BGP or OSPF. |
| **Segments and addressing** | Create your segments, manage DHCP, DNS and IP address management. |
| **Security** | Manage the distributed firewall (DFW), groups, context profiles and intrusion detection. |
| **Services** | Configure NAT, load balancing and VPNs. |
| **Troubleshooting** | Use Traceflow, port mirroring, IPFIX and statistics. |

### What stays managed by OVHcloud

Some rights are given to none of your users, because they touch the platform itself or would allow the protections of your Workload Domain to be bypassed.

| What stays with OVHcloud | Why |
|---|---|
| **Permissions and roles** | A role able to grant rights could also lift the protections described here. |
| **Host configuration**: host profiles, updates, distributed switch, key servers | It is the foundation of the platform, which OVHcloud operates and updates. |
| **Encryption key management** and encryption policies | A deleted key would leave every VM it encrypts unreadable. |
| **vCenter extensions and service accounts** | An extension acts on the whole vCenter, and a service account would allow acting under another identity. |
| **Alarm and scheduled task scripts** | They run on the vCenter appliance itself. |
| **Edges and edge clusters** | They are created, grown and removed from the OVHcloud Control Panel. |
| **NSX administration**: system, users, roles, transport nodes | It stays with OVHcloud. |

vSphere Kubernetes Service (VKS) is not offered: the matching rights are not given.

### Protected objects

Some objects in your vCenter belong to the platform. They are protected by their own permissions, which apply to both of your access levels:

| Object | Access |
|---|---|
| **NSX Edge VMs** | None: they do not appear in your inventory. |
| **Platform port groups**: management, vMotion and vSAN, distributed switch uplinks, `Edge-Trunk`, `Public-VLAN` | None. |
| **vSAN witness folder** (3-AZ) | Read-only: you can see the witness, without being able to change it. |

:::warning
OVHcloud reapplies this rights model continuously. A permission added or changed outside the model is removed at the next pass: do not rely on a manual change to the permissions of your vCenter or NSX.
:::
