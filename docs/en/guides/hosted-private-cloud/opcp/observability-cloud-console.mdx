## Objective

**This guide shows Organization users how to open the Observability service from the OPCP Cloud Console, explore their logs, metrics and traces, and set up dashboards and alerts.**

The **Observability** service gives your Organization its own Grafana, with its own logs, metrics, traces and alerts. No other Organization can see your data. You sign in with your Organization account.

How your IT Admin deploys the service is described in the guide [Deploying the Observability service](/guides/hosted-private-cloud/opcp/observability-admin-console).

This guide documents version `0.1.0-alpha.99`.

## Requirements

- An account in your Organization, as described in the guide [OPCP Cloud Console](/guides/hosted-private-cloud/opcp/cloud-console-getting-started).
- The Observability service enabled for your Organization by your IT Admin.

## Instructions

### What the service offers in the Alpha

| Feature | In the Alpha |
|---------|--------------|
| Grafana for your Organization, with single sign-on | Yes |
| Logs, metrics and traces of your Organization | Yes |
| **IAM audit log**: sign-ins and IAM changes of your Organization | Yes, unless your IT Admin turned it off |
| **Usage & Limits**: your usage against the limits of your Organization | Yes |
| Your own dashboards and alert rules, notifications to your tools | Yes |
| Data and access per project | No: everything is per Organization, see [Known limitations](#known-limitations) |
| Sending the data of your own applications | No, see [Known limitations](#known-limitations) |

### Opening the service

1. Log in to the OPCP Cloud Console and open **Observability** in your Enabled Services.
2. In the <code className="action">Overview</code> tab, open the `grafana_url` link.
3. Sign in with your Organization account.

Every user of the Organization who signs in gets the **Editor** role in Grafana: they can explore the data and create and change dashboards and alert rules.

### Exploring your data

In the Alpha, the data of your Organization comes from the IAM audit log of your Keycloak realm. The other Cloud Store services add their data, for example the S3 access logs of your buckets, with the Beta.

Four data sources are provisioned in your Grafana:

| Data source | Contents | Query language |
|-------------|----------|----------------|
| **Loki** (default) | Logs | LogQL |
| **Mimir** | Metrics | PromQL |
| **Tempo** | Traces | TraceQL |
| **Alertmanager** | Your alerts and notification settings | — |

To explore your data:

1. Open <code className="action">Explore</code> in the Grafana menu and select a data source.
2. Build the query with the **Builder** mode, or type it in **Code** mode. For example:
    - Loki: `{job="keycloak-audit", type="LOGIN_ERROR"}` returns the failed sign-ins of your Organization;
    - Mimir: open the **Metrics browser** to list the metrics your Organization has, then pick one.
3. Select the time range and click <code className="action">Run query</code>.

To create your own dashboard, go to <code className="action">Dashboards</code> > <code className="action">New</code>. You can also import a dashboard from a JSON file with <code className="action">Dashboards</code> > <code className="action">New</code> > <code className="action">Import</code>.

### Data retention and limits

Your IT Admin sets how long your data is kept. By default:

| Data | Default retention |
|------|-------------------|
| Logs | 30 days |
| Metrics | 365 days |
| Traces | 30 days |

Your Organization has fixed limits:

| Signal | Limit |
|--------|-------|
| Metrics | 150,000 active series, 15,000 samples per second |
| Logs | 1 MiB/s (burst 4 MiB), 5,000 active log streams |
| Traces | 2 MiB/s (burst 4 MiB) |

Data above a rate limit is refused and sent again later by its sender. Metric series above 150,000 are dropped. Follow your usage on the **Usage & Limits** dashboard.

### Setting up alerts

You can write alert rules on your logs and metrics, and send the notifications to your own tools.

1. Go to <code className="action">Alerting</code> > <code className="action">Contact points</code> and click <code className="action">Add contact point</code>. Choose the integration (email, webhook, Slack, Microsoft Teams, PagerDuty, etc.) and fill in its settings.
2. Go to <code className="action">Alerting</code> > <code className="action">Alert rules</code> and click <code className="action">New alert rule</code>.
3. Choose the **Mimir** or **Loki** data source, write the query and the condition, and choose your contact point.
4. Save the rule.

The rules are evaluated by the service itself, so they keep running when nobody has Grafana open.

:::info
When you write a rule, the preview may show the error `Jwt is missing`. Save the rule anyway: it is evaluated correctly. Check the result in <code className="action">Explore</code>.

:::

:::warning
Notifications can only be sent to public addresses. A contact point that targets a private address (for example `10.x.x.x` or `192.168.x.x`) fails with `blocked address`.

:::

### Known limitations

The Alpha has the following known limitations:

| Limitation | Detail |
|------------|--------|
| Everything is per Organization | All users of the Organization see all its data and are **Editor**. Data, roles and dashboards per project come with the Beta. |
| No data from your own applications | Sending the logs and metrics of your own applications is not offered in the Alpha. It comes with the Beta. |
| No forwarding, no export | Sending a copy of your data to an external platform, and a full export, come with the Beta. |
| Few data sources | In the Alpha, only the IAM audit log sends data to your Organization. The Cloud Store services, for example Object Storage, send theirs with the Beta. |
| Retention | Only your IT Admin can change the retention of your data. |
| Short query errors | During upgrades or restarts of the service, queries over the last hours may fail for a short time. No data is lost: run the query again. |
