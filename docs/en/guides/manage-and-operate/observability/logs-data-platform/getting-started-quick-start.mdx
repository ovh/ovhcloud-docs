## Objective

Welcome to the quick start tutorial of the Logs Data Platform. This Quick start guide will help you to understand the core concepts behind the Logs Data Platform and how to send your first logs to the engine.

[[cpnav:logs-data-platform|en]]

## Instructions

Creating a Logs Data Platform account is free: with its pay-as-you-go pricing you pay only for what you use.

{/* CP-STEPS-START:cp-access */}

To configure your account, use OVHcloud IAM to enable [IAM identities](/guides/account-and-service-management/account-information/ovhcloud-users-management) and [IAM policies](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/iam-access-management). IAM manages all security aspects of Logs Data Platform.

Once IAM is enabled, open the **Observability** section of the OVHcloud Console. It opens on the **Service management** page of your Logs Data Platform service:

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Service management page of a Logs Data Platform service in the OVHcloud Console" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/console/service-management-information.png" loading="lazy" />

Every page of the **Observability** section starts with a **Service** list, where you choose the Logs Data Platform service you want to work on, with **Add a new service** beside it. Your Logs Data Platform service name is shown in this list, and as the **ID** in the **General information** section of the **Service management** page, together with its URN. This service name is used in the IAM URN of the Logs Data Platform service like this:


```text
urn:v1:eu:resource:ldp:ldp-ag-03980
```


In the left menu, the **Observability** section holds five entries: **Dashboards**, **Logs Explorer**, **Logs Management**, **Alerting** and **Service management**.

The **Service management** page gathers the settings of your service in three tabs: **General information**, **Usage** and **IAM Policies**.

- The **General information** tab shows the display name, status, type of service, creation date, ID and URN of your service. Click the pencil icon next to **Display name** to change the displayed name of your account, which can be useful in the case you have multiple accounts tied to the same OVHcloud account (NIC handle).
- The **IAM Policies** tab allows you to modify access rights for your content.

The **Logs Management** page holds the main items of your service, in four tabs:

1. The **Data streams** are the recipients of your logs. When you send a log with the correct stream token, it arrives automatically in your stream in a powerful software named Graylog. When you access your stream in Graylog you will be able to search your logs and immediately analyze them. Each data stream has its own page: the **Connection info** section shows your assigned access point (the endpoint), and the **Test stream** section lists the available ports for each supported format. The **Data gathering tool** tab of a data stream allows you to request OVHcloud to host your own dedicated collector such as Logstash or Flowgger.
2. The **Encryption Keys** tab holds your PGP encryption keys for [cold storage archive](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/archive-cold-storage). More on that in the [dedicated documentation](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/cold-storage-encryption).
3. The **OpenSearch indexes** tab allows you to create your dedicated OpenSearch Index or retrieve the ones used to store your OpenSearch Dashboards settings.
4. The **OpenSearch aliases** tab provides access to your data directly from your OpenSearch Dashboards or using an OpenSearch query.

The **Dashboards** page groups your dashboards in two tabs:

1. The **Graylog dashboards** tab lists your Dashboards. A Dashboard provides a global view of your logs. It is an efficient way to analyse your logs and to view high-level metrics and trends without being overwhelmed by the log details.
2. The **OpenSearch Dashboards instances** tab creates your personal OpenSearch Dashboards instance, in order to exploit the aliases and index from the powerful OpenSearch Dashboards interface.

### Let's send some logs!

{/* CP-STEPS-START:send-logs-1 */}

- First, create a stream in order to get your token: on the **Logs Management** page, in the **Data streams** tab, click <code className="action">Add data stream</code>. You will be redirected to a page where you can add a name and a description for the stream:

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Add data stream page with the name and description fields" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/console/add-data-stream.png" loading="lazy" />

On the same page, you can enable the live-tail websocket feature. Doing so allows the indexation of your logs to explore them in Graylog, choose the retention of the data in this stream, and limit the amount of logs stored in this stream to control your budget.

- Once you have done this, click <code className="action">Create</code>. You have created your first stream. It appears in the **Data streams** list. Click its name to open the stream page, where you can copy the X-OVH-TOKEN token, shown as the write token in the **Connection info** section. This value is the only token you will need to route logs to your stream.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Data streams list with the three-dot menu of a stream open" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/console/data-streams-actions-menu.png" loading="lazy" />

The `⋮` button in the **Actions** column of each stream gives you several features:

- **Edit** allows you to edit the name and the description of your stream.
- **Copy the write token** retrieves your token and lets you use it in your various log collectors.
- **Access Graylog** gives you direct access to your stream and its logs.
- **Live monitor** allows you to see the logs incoming into your stream in real-time. Note that Graylog also provides this functionality. On this page you can also **Test** different log formats from your computer to your stream. [Learn more about live monitoring](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/ldp-tail)
- **Manage alerts** allows you to define your alert conditions on the logs routed to the stream. [Learn more about alerting](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/alerting)
- **Manage archives** allows you to download the cold stored archives. [Learn more about cold storage](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/archive-cold-storage)
- **Delete** removes your stream from the system and all related content.

{/* CP-STEPS-END:send-logs-1 */}

Logs Data Platform supports several logs formats, each one of them has its own advantages and disadvantages. Here are the different formats available

- **GELF**: This is the native format of logs used by Graylog. This JSON format will allow you to send logs very easily. See: [https://go2docs.graylog.org/4-x/getting_in_log_data/gelf.html?tocpath=Getting%20in%20Log%20Data%7CLog%20Sources%7CGELF%7C_____0#GELFPayloadSpecification](https://go2docs.graylog.org/4-x/getting_in_log_data/gelf.html?tocpath=Getting%20in%20Log%20Data%7CLog%20Sources%7CGELF%7C_____0#GELFPayloadSpecification). The GELF input only accepts a null (`\0`) delimiter.
- **LTSV**: This simple format is very efficient and is still human readable. You can learn more about it [here](http://ltsv.org). LTSV has two inputs that accept a line delimiter or a null delimiter.
- **RFC 5424**: This format is commonly used by logs utilities such as syslog. It is extensible enough to allow you to send all your data. More information about it can be found at this link: [RFC 5424](https://tools.ietf.org/html/rfc5424).
- **Cap'n'Proto**: The most efficient log format. This is a binary format that allows you to maintain a low footprint and high speed performance. For more information, check out the official website: [Cap'n'Proto](https://capnproto.org/).
- **Beats**: A secure and reliable protocol used by the beats family in the Elasticsearch ecosystem (Ex: [Filebeat](/guides/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/filebeat-logs), [Metricbeat](https://www.elastic.co/beats/metricbeat), [Winlogbeat](https://www.elastic.co/beats/winlogbeat)).

Here are the ports you can use on your cluster to send your logs. You can either use the secured ones with SSL Enabled (TLS >= 1.2) or use the plain unsecured ones if you can't use an SSL transport.

||Syslog RFC5424|Gelf|LTSV line|LTSV nul|Cap’n’Proto|Beats|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|TCP/TLS|6514|12202|12201|12200|12204|5044|
|TCP|514|2202|2201|2200|2204|---|
|UDP|514|2202|2201|2200|2204|---|

As said before, you can retrieve the address and the ports of your cluster on the page of each data stream: click the name of your stream in the **Data streams** list, then find the endpoint in the **Connection info** section and the address and port for each log format in the **Test stream** section.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Data stream page showing the Connection info and Test stream sections" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/console/data-stream-connection-info.png" loading="lazy" />

To send your logs to Logs Data Platform you can easily test your stream by doing, for example, a simple `echo` followed by an `openssl` command. Here are 3 examples, choose the format you like the most with your preferred terminal:  Note that each format has its own timestamp format: GELF uses [seconds from epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_time), RFC 5424 and LTSV use the [RFC 3339](https://tools.ietf.org/html/rfc3339). Don't forget to change the **timestamp** to your current time to see your logs (by default Graylog displays recent logs; you can change the search scope using the top‑left time picker. Also, ensure you replace the **token** with the correct one.

*GELF*:

```bash
$ ubuntu@server:~$ echo -e '&#123;"version":"1.1",  "_X-OVH-TOKEN":"d93eee2a-697f-4bac-a452-705416b98a04", "host": "example.org", "short_message": "A short message that helps you identify what is going on", "full_message": "Backtrace here\n\nmore stuff", "timestamp": 1385053862.3072, "level": 1, "_user_id": 9001, "_some_info": "foo", "some_metric_num": 42.0 &#125;\0' | \
openssl s_client -quiet -no_ign_eof -connect `<your_cluster>`.logs.ovh.com:12202
```

For this format, the time is in seconds, with optional milliseconds as decimals.

*LTSV*:

```bash
$ ubuntu@server:~$ echo -e 'X-OVH-TOKEN:d93eee2a-697f-4bac-a452-705416b98a04\thost:example.org\ttime:2016-03-08T14:44:01+01:00\tmessage:A short message that helps you identify what is going on\tfull_message:Backtrace here\n\nmore stuff\tlevel:1\tuser_id:9001\tsome_info:foo\tsome_metric_num:42.0\0'| \
openssl s_client -quiet -no_ign_eof -connect `<your_cluster>`.logs.ovh.com:12200
```

For this format the time is in the RFC 3339 format.

*RFC 5424*:

```bash
$ ubuntu@server:~$ echo -e '&lt;6>1 2016-03-08T14:44:01+01:00 149.202.165.20 example.org - - [exampleSDID@8485 X-OVH-TOKEN="d93eee2a-697f-4bac-a452-705416b98a04" user_id="9001"  some_info="foo" some_metric_num="42.0" ] A short message that helps you identify what is going on\n' | \
openssl s_client -quiet -no_ign_eof -connect `<your_cluster>`.logs.ovh.com:6514
```

For this format the time is in the RFC 3339 format.

- To see your logs in Graylog, click the `⋮` button in the **Actions** column of your stream in the **Data streams** list. Click **Access Graylog** to jump straight to Graylog.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Data streams list with the Access Graylog entry in the stream menu" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/console/data-streams-actions-menu.png" loading="lazy" />

The Graylog login page looks like this:

<img className="thumbnail" alt="login Graylog" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/login.png" loading="lazy" />

On this page you can select the Identity Provider available to this cluster. Select the right provider for your service and you will be redirected to the OVHcloud login page or, if you are already logged, you will land on the confirmation page.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="confirmation" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/confirmation.png" loading="lazy" />

Once logged, you will be redirected to this page:

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Graylog Stream" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/graylog-stream.png" loading="lazy" />

On this page you can already search for the different values present in the logs by using the search bar (at the top of the page). You can also select the time range of your search by playing with time picker at the top left of the page.
For example to search all the logs in the last 5 minutes that contain `42` for the value `some_metric_num` you can enter in the search bar after you have select **Last 5 minutes** in the time picker combobox at the top:

```
some_metric_num:42
```

Press <code className="action">Enter</code> or click on the Green button to launch the search and wait for your results.

It's possible to search some part of your message by entering:

```
helps going
```

Giving you all the messages that contains the terms `helps` and `going`.

Graylog allows you to extensively search through your logs without compromising usability. For more information about how to craft relevant searches on Graylog, please visit [Graylog Search Documentation](https://go2docs.graylog.org/4-x/making_sense_of_your_log_data/writing_search_queries.html).

Send several logs with different values for `user_id`, for example. At the left of the page you will see the fields present in your stream, you can click on the `user_id` checkbox to display all the values for this field along the logs.

### Let's create a Dashboard

{/* CP-STEPS-START:create-dashboard */}

Go back to the OVHcloud Console. You will now create a Dashboard that will allow you to explore your data in a graphical manner. It is even simpler to create a Dashboard: on the **Dashboards** page, in the **Graylog dashboards** tab, click <code className="action">Add a dashboard</code> and on the next page, add a description and a title for your Dashboard. Once created, you can use the `⋮` button of your dashboard, then **Graylog access**, to access it immediately.

<img className="thumbnail" alt="Graylog dashboards tab with the Add a dashboard button and a dashboard menu" src="/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/console/graylog-dashboards.png" loading="lazy" />

{/* CP-STEPS-END:create-dashboard */}

Your dashboard is empty at first; we will now add some widgets to it. To do that, get back to your stream: you can use the link on graylog (under the Stream tab) or the link on your console as you wish. Let's say you want all the user Ids for which the value `some_metric_num` is above `30`, first. You search for this data:

- In the search bar, enter the following: `some_metric_num:>30`
- Above the search bar, select the relative time range you want to use in your widgets. If you want the widget to display the last hour, select  **Search in the last Hour**
- On the left panel, click on the button that looks like an "X" to open the fields menu.
- Unroll the `user_id` menu by clicking on the value and select  **Show top values**. It will then display a nice widget with the distribution of the most frequent `user_ids`.

You can <code className="action">edit</code> the widget by using its top right menu arrow. For example you can change the visualization type by choosing **Pie Chart** in the top left _Visualization Type_ option.

![Quick Values](/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/quick-values.png)

This widget gives you the most frequent `user_id` in the logs of the last hour that have a `some_metric_num` value above `30`.

- To add this really critical information to your dashboard, click on the <code className="action">Copy to Dashboard</code> menu button and select your newly created Dashboard. After that, you will be redirected to the Dashboard with your newly created widget in it.

![critical Dashboard](/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/critical-dashboard.png)

Mixing various widgets on the same dashboard gives this feature all its sense. To add a widget in this Dashboard you can also directly use the "+" button on the left panel. This button will ask you the type of widget you want to create (Aggregation or Message Count or Message Table). The Aggregation option allows you to create various visualization types for your data.

![widget creation](/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/widget-create.png)

In this screenshot, you can see that we added a widget that represents the mean values for some_metric_num (by using generate chart instead of quick values for the field some_metric in the stream tab).

![critical Dashboard 2](/images/manage-and-operate/observability/logs-data-platform/getting-started-quick-start/critical-dashboard-2.png)

Note that dashboards are interactive and you can use the top search bar and the date picker to display only the widgets matching a particular type of event.
